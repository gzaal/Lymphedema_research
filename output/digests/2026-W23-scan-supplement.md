**pm_41589511 — HLTS (SOX18) + von Willebrand Factor Deficiency** ⚑ *Clinically actionable in HLTS*

*Am J Med Genetics, June 2026 | Kanno M et al.*

**What happened:** A case report describes a 10-year-old boy with Hypotrichosis-Lymphedema-Telangiectasia Syndrome (HLTS; SOX18 variant c.481C>T, p.Gln161Ter) who presented with iron-deficiency anemia and recurrent mucosal bleeding from unexpectedly reduced von Willebrand factor (vWF) levels. No VWF gene mutation was found — the reduction appears secondary to SOX18 dysfunction, consistent with SOX18's role in vWF expression in haematopoietic stem cells. vWF replacement therapy enabled safe dental procedures.

**Why people may care:** This is the first published case linking HLTS/SOX18 to haemostatic deficiency. If vWF deficiency is a recurrent feature of HLTS, it has direct pre-procedural implications: clinicians managing HLTS patients (a primary lymphedema syndrome) should consider haemostatic screening before elective invasive procedures.

**What limits confidence:** Single case — prevalence across the HLTS population is unknown. The mechanistic link from SOX18 to vWF reduction is plausible but not directly validated in this patient. The concurrent aortic valve regurgitation may reflect broader SOX18-related vascular dysregulation.

**pm_42223480 — Aquaporin-1 as an Inflammation-Specific Lymphangiogenesis Regulator**

*J Experimental Medicine, July 2026 (ahead-of-print) | Roci I, Kim J, Petrova TV et al.*

**What happened:** A multi-lab study using murine scRNA-seq and conditional AQP1 deletion found that aquaporin-1 is required for lymphatic endothelial cell migration under hyperosmotic stress, preserving cytoskeletal and junctional remodeling. AQP1 is upregulated in human lymphedema tissue and lymphatic malformations — but not during embryonic lymphangiogenesis — suggesting it marks a distinct inflammatory/regenerative lymphangiogenic program. AQP1 deletion impaired intestinal lacteal function and dietary lipid uptake.

**Why people may care:** AQP1 defines a novel, inflammation-specific lymphangiogenic program that is active in lymphedema and lymphatic malformations (both in scope). The distinction from embryonic lymphangiogenesis means AQP1-dependent pathways may be targetable in disease without disrupting development. This is a JEM-quality mechanistic study with both mouse and human tissue data.

**What limits confidence:** Primarily mouse intestinal lacteal biology — the relevance to peripheral lymphatics in primary lymphedema (lower-limb, arm, genital) is indirect. Human tissue data shows association only; AQP1 upregulation in lymphedema could be reactive rather than causative. No therapeutic intervention was studied.

**pm_42220462 — Omentum Activates FOXC2/PROX1/VEGF-C Cascade in Murine Lymphedema Model**

*Archives of Plastic Surgery, May 2026 | Hojo M et al.*

**What happened:** Activated omental tissue in mice upregulates HIF-1α → Ang2 → FOXC2 and PROX1 expression. Omental-conditioned supernatant reduced peak edema, accelerated resolution, and increased lymphatic vessel density in a murine hindlimb lymphedema model, with reduced macrophage infiltration.

**Why people may care:** FOXC2 (lymphedema-distichiasis gene) and PROX1 are core primary lymphedema genes; their activation by an omental paracrine mechanism provides a biological rationale for omentum-based vascularized lymph node transfer approaches, and suggests VEGF-C secretion as a key mediator.

**What limits confidence:** Mouse model with poor translational history in lymphedema. Conditioned supernatant injection is not equivalent to surgical omentum transfer. FOXC2/PROX1 upregulation in recipient tissue is context-specific and cannot correct underlying genetic mutations causing primary lymphedema.

**pm_42001630 — LVA vs. VLNT Retrospective Comparison, n=151, Lower Limb**

*JPRAS, June 2026 | Seidenstuecker K, Kappos EA et al.*

**What happened:** Retrospective cohort of 151 lower limb lymphedema patients (123 LVA, 28 VLNT), minimum 12 months follow-up (2015–2023). LVA showed rapid effectiveness gains within the first 3 months; VLNT showed slower but progressive improvement throughout 12 months, narrowing the gap. Complication rate: 7.1% (2/28) for VLNT, 0% for LVA.

**Why people may care:** Provides comparative trajectory data between the two dominant microsurgical options — LVA for early/fast gains, VLNT for slower but potentially durable improvement. Relevant for surgical planning discussions, though the study has no conservative management comparator.

**What limits confidence:** Retrospective design; unequal group sizes (VLNT n=28 is underpowered for comparative conclusions); 12-month maximum follow-up in a chronic condition; lymphedema type not specified (likely predominantly secondary); obesity/BMI changes not controlled; no RCT comparator — the standard surgical evidence caveat applies.

**pm_42013481 — Lymph-Interpositional Flap Transfer (LIFT): Rabbit Model of Lymph Axiality**

*JPRAS, June 2026 | Lee J, Yoon ES et al.*

**What happened:** In a rabbit hindlimb model, lymph-interpositional flap transfer with maintained directional lymphatic alignment (LIFT+) reduced limb volume at week 3 and achieved higher ICG lymphography reconnection rates versus misaligned controls. VEGF-C and podoplanin were elevated; TGF-β was reduced.

**Why people may care:** Provides preclinical support for the "lymph axiality" principle — that directional alignment of lymphatic channels during surgical reconstruction matters for functional reconnection. The molecular correlates (VEGF-C upregulation, TGF-β reduction) support this interpretation.

**What limits confidence:** Rabbit model; 3-week assessment is clinically meaningless for a chronic condition; directional alignment is difficult to standardise in human surgery; no human data; lymphedema has a poor preclinical-to-clinical translation record.

## Skepticism Notes

1. **AQP1 (pm_42223480):** The human tissue data (upregulation in lymphedema/LM) is associative. The primary evidence base is mouse gut lacteals — a specialised vascular bed. Do not extrapolate lymphangiogenic findings from intestinal lacteals to peripheral lymphatics without independent validation.

2. **LVA vs. VLNT (pm_42001630):** The VLNT group (n=28) is underpowered for the conclusions drawn. The comparison is purely between surgical options — the clinically relevant question (surgery vs. conservative therapy) is unanswered by this study. The absence of a conservative arm is the most important limitation.

3. **Omentum/FOXC2 (pm_42220462):** FOXC2 and PROX1 upregulation in treated mice does not mean these transcription factors are expressed at levels sufficient to functionally rescue lymphatic development, nor that this effect would occur in patients with pathogenic FOXC2 mutations. The observation is interesting but the translational inference should be held lightly.

4. **HLTS/vWF (pm_41589511):** One patient. The clinical takeaway (consider vWF screening in HLTS before procedures) is reasonable and low-risk, but the prevalence of vWF deficiency across the HLTS population is entirely unknown. Do not represent this as established HLTS management guidance.
