## Highlights

### Highlight 1: KRAS-G12D Drives Lymphatic Valve Loss via MMP Upregulation — A Druggable Mechanism in KRAS-Mutant Lymphatic Malformations

- **What happened:** Bowles et al. (*Arteriosclerosis, Thrombosis, and Vascular Biology*, 2026; alerted by 2026-05-09 scan) used Flt4CreERT2;Kras-LSL-G12D;Prox1GFP mice and KRAS-G12D-expressing human dermal lymphatic endothelial cells to dissect why somatic KRAS mutations cause valve loss, chylous effusions, and chylothorax in patients with lymphatic malformations. Lymphatic-restricted KRAS-G12D significantly attenuated valve development across mesentery, diaphragm, and ear skin. KRAS-G12D LECs upregulated matrix metalloproteinases (MMPs); pharmacologic MMP inhibition rescued valve formation in 3D culture.
- **Why people may care:** Somatic KRAS mutations are a recognised driver of complex lymphatic anomalies (CLA), chylothorax, and chylous ascites. This work identifies a tractable downstream enzyme class (MMPs) that could in principle be targeted to rescue valve function — orthogonal to the current mTOR (sirolimus) and emerging MEK/RAS-pathway (trametinib) options for these patients.
- **What limits confidence:** Mouse cKO and 3D cell culture only; no patient tissue or organoid validation; no in vivo MMP-inhibitor rescue in a vertebrate KRAS lymphatic-malformation model; broad MMP inhibition has historically been toxic in oncology trials. Lymphedema preclinical-to-clinical translation has a poor track record (preclinical translation warning).
- **Classification:** hypothesis-generating
- **Scores:** Importance 7/10 | Evidence 5/10 | Novelty 7/10 | Decision 4/10 | Calibration 9/10

### Highlight 2: Subclinical Central Lymphatic Abnormalities Detected in Noonan Syndrome by MR-Lymphangiography

- **What happened:** Sleboda et al. (*American Journal of Medical Genetics Part A*, 2026) reported a controlled cross-sectional imaging study comparing 10 Noonan-syndrome individuals (4 with peripheral lymphedema, 6 without) to 10 age- and sex-matched cardiovascularly healthy controls using non-contrast T2 MR-lymphangiography, near-infrared fluorescence imaging, and strain-gauge plethysmography. 89% (8/9) of Noonan individuals showed thoracic-duct morphology variations vs 20% (2/10) of controls (p<0.05). Central lymphatic abnormalities were present in 5/9 Noonan individuals — including 3 without overt clinical lymphatic disease. Abnormal peripheral collective vessel conduction (retrograde or absent flow) was observed only in Noonan individuals with overt lymphedema.
- **Why people may care:** Noonan syndrome is the most common single-gene RASopathy with lymphatic involvement (chylothorax, lymphedema, persistent fetal hydrops). The signal that central lymphatic abnormalities may be common — and may pre-date overt limb disease — is relevant for pre-cardiac-surgery counselling and for understanding why some Noonan patients develop refractory effusions. Cross-references the Liu 2026 finding of central-lymphatic imaging signal in non-syndromic primary lymphedema (n=279).
- **What limits confidence:** Small sample (n=10 Noonan vs 10 controls); cross-sectional (no longitudinal change or treatment-outcome data); surrogate endpoint (imaging morphology). No genotype-stratified analysis (PTPN11 vs SOS1 vs RAF1 etc.) given the small n. Does **not** yet justify routine central lymphatic MR screening for all Noonan patients.
- **Classification:** hypothesis-generating (toward practice-relevant if replicated and outcome-linked)
- **Scores:** Importance 6/10 | Evidence 5/10 | Novelty 7/10 | Decision 4/10 | Calibration 9/10

### Highlight 3: Lymphedema Is Among the Strongest Population-Level Risk Factors for First-Episode Lower-Leg Cellulitis (TriNetX, large EHR cohort)

- **What happened:** A large retrospective TriNetX EHR cohort (Carls et al., *International Journal of Infectious Diseases*, 2026) identified independent risk factors for first-episode lower-leg cellulitis. **Lymphedema** had a hazard ratio of **1.631 (95% CI 1.584–1.680, p<0.0001)** — the second-largest single risk factor, exceeded only by leg ulcers (HR 2.669). Other independent risk factors: heart failure, venous insufficiency / varicosis, nicotine, alcohol, diabetes, onychomycosis, tinea pedis, obesity, corticosteroids, low SES, atopic dermatitis, male sex.
- **Why people may care:** Reinforces the long-standing clinical message — lymphedema is itself a major cellulitis risk factor and skin/nail care, tinea pedis treatment, and prophylactic penicillin in recurrent cases are not "nice-to-haves." The HR estimate is from one of the largest EHR cohorts published for this question.
- **What limits confidence:** EHR-defined lymphedema includes a heterogeneous mix of primary and secondary cases; primary-lymphedema-specific risk is not separately reported. Retrospective; potential for surveillance bias (patients with documented lymphedema may have more healthcare contact). The HR confirms a known association — it is reinforcement, not new biology.
- **Classification:** background/context (with practice-reinforcing message)
- **Scores:** Importance 5/10 | Evidence 5/10 | Novelty 4/10 | Decision 4/10 | Calibration 9/10

## Evolving Understanding

### Genetics — KRAS adds a new "downstream-druggable" axis to the lymphatic-malformation playbook

The KRAS-G12D / MMP work (Bowles 2026) is mechanistically the most consequential primary-lymphedema-relevant paper of W19. KRAS somatic mutations are a recognised driver of CLA, chylothorax, and chylous ascites. Until now, the mainstay of pharmacotherapy in KRAS-mutant lymphatic disease has been MEK pathway inhibition (e.g., trametinib) which targets RAS signalling itself. The MMP axis is downstream and offers an orthogonal target — but the data are entirely preclinical, the rescue experiment is in 3D culture, and broad MMP inhibition has a difficult oncology history. *Confidence change:* a new finding `finding_kras_mmp_valve_loss_2026` is added at low confidence (preclinical only).

### Genetics — central lymphatic imaging signal extends from non-syndromic to RASopathy primary lymphatic disease

The Sleboda 2026 Noonan paper builds on the Liu 2026 *Vascular Medicine* MRTD work in non-syndromic primary lower-extremity lymphedema (n=279). The two cohorts are very different in design — Liu was large, Liu was non-syndromic; Sleboda is small (n=10) but explicitly controlled and includes Noonan individuals **without** clinical lymphatic disease. Together they support a coherent claim: central lymphatic morphologic abnormalities are a sensitive imaging readout in primary lymphatic biology, in both non-syndromic and syndromic populations. *Confidence change:* a new finding `finding_noonan_central_lymphatics_2026` is added at low_to_moderate confidence; `finding_mri_central_lymphatics_primary_2026` is updated with a Noonan supporting paper and an evolution log entry but its core confidence remains low_to_moderate pending outcome-linked validation.

### Surgical evidence gap — reinforced, not closed

Two W19 papers reinforce `finding_surgical_evidence_gap_global` rather than weaken it:
- pm_42028378 (JPRAS Open bibliometric of the 50 most-cited lymphatic surgery papers) confirms case series are the most common design and OCEBM Level 4-5 evidence dominates.
- pm_42085896 (J Surg Res VLNT recipient-site retrospective, n=51 mixed PLE+SLE) is another single-center retrospective comparative with surrogate volume endpoints. The PLE-specific multilevel inset signal is interesting and consistent with the pre-existing observation that combined LVA+VLNT or multi-recipient strategies may outperform single-site approaches in the right patients — but this is hypothesis-generating, not practice-changing.

Confidence in the surgical evidence gap finding remains high; the evolution log is updated with both W19 papers.

### Pediatric lymphatic anomaly management — practice consolidation continues

Three W19 pediatric-LM items (pm_41830665 H&N 10y cohort, pm_42065150 PPF vs PYM sclerotherapy, pm_41147663 JEADV targeted-therapy review) consolidate the imaging-led, genotype-aware multidisciplinary model already described in the W18 KB05 update (Klein 2026 *Pediatr Radiol*). Real-world uptake numbers from pm_41830665 are striking: even at a tertiary BVAP program over a decade, **molecular testing was performed in only 13.6% of pediatric H&N LM patients** (although yield was 100% pathogenic in those tested), and **sirolimus was used in 25.4%** with onward transition to alpelisib in 4 cases. The gap between the consensus framework and bedside uptake remains large. No finding update; KB05 is updated with this real-world signal.

### Genetic testing access — first cross-center quantitative analysis

pm_42093915 (n=421, two academic centers) is the first systematic quantification of how somatic genetic testing for vascular anomalies actually flows through prior authorization. **84% of PA requests approved, 93% completed, 70% positive (P/LP).** Sample type/freshness did not affect yield. Skin findings and male sex predicted PA initiation; higher Area Deprivation Index reduced PA odds at one center. This is access/workflow data — not a finding update — but it supports the KB03 "genetic testing in practice" message that yield is high when testing is actually performed, and that access barriers are real and partly socioeconomic.

### Cellulitis risk — large-cohort confirmation of a long-known association

pm_41722760 (TriNetX cohort, lymphedema HR 1.631) confirms the population-level risk relationship. No finding update; KB05 cellulitis section is updated with the HR estimate as a citable reference.

## Skepticism Notes

1. **Tie2 / ABTAA + VLNT — preclinical, not therapeutic.** pm_40991251 frames antibody-mediated Tie2 activation as enhancing VLNT outcomes. Mouse hindlimb LE model only; surgical-secondary lymphedema in mice is a poor proxy for primary lymphedema; ABTAA is not in human lymphedema trials. Treat as research-prioritisation lead at most.

2. **VLNT recipient-site retrospective — over-reaches what 51 mixed patients can support.** pm_42085896 reports a multilevel-inset benefit only in the PLE subgroup, derived from a single-center retrospective with surrogate volume endpoints. Consistent with prior LVA+VLNT combined-procedure literature, but no RCT.

3. **Pediatric sclerotherapy comparison — retrospective non-randomised allocation.** pm_42065150 (PPF vs PYM) reports superior first-session response and pain relief with PPF. Allocation is non-random and the comparator is the same drug without the foam. Useful but retrospective.

4. **Targeted-therapy review (JEADV) — restate, not replace.** pm_41147663 is a useful synthesis of mTOR/PIK3CA/MEK targeted therapy for vascular anomalies but is narrative, not a guideline. Treat as background.

5. **Bibliometric ≠ evidence elevation.** pm_42028378 confirms the field is dominated by Level 4-5 evidence — an important meta-observation but not a clinical signal in itself.

6. **EHR-based lymphedema cohorts — primary-lymphedema cases are a minority.** Both the W18 MVP GWAS (pm_42017216) and this week's TriNetX cellulitis cohort (pm_41722760) use EHR-defined lymphedema. Primary-lymphedema-specific subgroup analyses are not reported. Apply estimates with caution to primary-lymphedema patients.

7. **PIK3CA/Trp53 mouse — relevance to primary lymphedema is indirect.** ss_841f40f8f6c4 is mostly a vascular-malformation tumour-biology paper; lymphatic involvement is incidental.

8. **Two corrections — not new evidence.** pm_41882370 and pm_42089974 are correction notices for prior items; tracker-maintenance only.
