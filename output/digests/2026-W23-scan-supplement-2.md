**pm_42235636 — Pneumatic Compression PRO Survey: n=10,543 Lower Extremity Lymphedema**

*Journal of Vascular Surgery: Venous and Lymphatic Disorders, June 2, 2026 | Raiker A, Dean S, Gasparis A, Labropoulos N*

**What happened:** A retrospective survey of 10,543 patients using pneumatic compression devices for lower extremity lymphedema found high device satisfaction that improved with usage frequency. Daily users reported the greatest benefits: swelling reduction (58%), heaviness relief (34%), and pain improvement (25%). Outcomes remained stable between 45- and 90-day assessments.

**Why people may care:** Large-scale patient-reported outcome data for pneumatic compression devices are uncommon. The frequency-benefit correlation provides population-level support for adherence counseling — a recognized management challenge — and suggests that device satisfaction scales with use, which may have implications for prescribing and insurance coverage discussions.

**What limits confidence:** No objective lymphedema measurements (no limb volume, circumference, L-Dex, or ICG data); no control group; survey respondents who completed 45–90 day assessments are inherently more adherent (selection bias); obesity and weight change not controlled; population is mixed primary and secondary lymphedema without subgroup data.

## Skepticism Notes

1. **Pneumatic compression survey (pm_42235636):** The correlation between device usage frequency and symptom benefit is observational and subject to reverse causation: patients with milder disease or better baseline function may find devices more tolerable and use them more, while more severely affected patients may use devices less due to discomfort or practical barriers. Benefit reported by daily users may partly reflect patient characteristics rather than the causal effect of daily use per se.

2. **Missing SS data:** Semantic Scholar was rate-limited this entire cycle.
