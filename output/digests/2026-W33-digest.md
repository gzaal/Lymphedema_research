## Highlights

**1. PLXND1 identified as a lymphatic mechanosensor required for valve development, with two pathogenic variants functionally characterised in primary lymphedema patients.**
- **What happened:** A *Journal of Clinical Investigation* study (July 2026) shows that conditional deletion of *Plxnd1* in mouse lymphatic endothelium causes major valve defects in two distinct lymphatic beds, and that PLXND1 acts as a mechanosensor within a "lymphatic mechanocomplex" that converts lymph flow into the valve transcriptional programme. Screening of primary lymphedema patients identified PLXND1 missense variants; two were functionally established as pathogenic, one disrupting ligand binding and one disrupting mechanosensing. Variants in other mechanocomplex members previously linked to lymphedema disrupted complex assembly.
- **Why people may care:** Most candidate-gene reports in this field stop at variant listing. This one separates two patient variants into distinct mechanistic classes and offers a framework in which several separately-discovered lymphedema genes converge on a single valve-formation complex — which is the kind of organising result the genetics of primary lymphedema currently lacks.
- **What limits confidence:** The abstract does not state how many patients were screened or how many carried variants; there is no replication cohort and no segregation data. The mechanism is mouse-derived, and lymphedema has a poor preclinical-to-clinical translation record. PLXND1 is a research candidate gene, not a diagnostic one, and there is no therapeutic implication.
- **Classification:** hypothesis-generating
- **Scores:** Importance 8/10 | Evidence 6/10 | Novelty 9/10 | Decision 5/10 | Calibration 8/10

**2. A MEK inhibitor failed in a RAS-pathway central conducting lymphatic anomaly — the first documented non-response entering this knowledge base's targeted-therapy record.**
- **What happened:** A patient with congenital heart disease and refractory post-operative chylothorax had imaging consistent with central conducting lymphatic anomaly and a SHOC2 variant of uncertain significance, p.(Ala308Val) — a RAS-MAPK scaffolding gene not previously described in CCLA. Zebrafish expressing the variant in lymphovenous endothelium developed pericardial edema, cystic caudal plexus malformations and thoracic duct dilation. Trametinib was trialled clinically **without improvement**, and immunoblotting suggested the variant acts through non-canonical mTOR signalling rather than canonical RAS-MAPK dysregulation.
- **Why people may care:** The sirolimus/trametinib evidence base for complex lymphatic anomalies is built almost entirely from published successes, and publication bias against non-responders in n=1 targeted-therapy reporting is severe. A documented pathway-variant-positive, drug-negative case is a useful counterweight, and it argues against the shorthand "RAS-pathway variant, therefore trametinib" when committing a critically ill patient to a targeted agent.
- **What limits confidence:** n=1. A single uncontrolled treatment failure demonstrates nothing about the drug's efficacy in general, the variant remains formally a VUS, and zebrafish transient overexpression is a weak pathogenicity assay. This does not refute the existing case-report literature; it complicates it.
- **Classification:** hypothesis-generating
- **Scores:** Importance 6/10 | Evidence 4/10 | Novelty 8/10 | Decision 4/10 | Calibration 8/10

**3. Systematic review finds lymphoedema management — especially compression — is the consistently supported non-antibiotic route to preventing recurrent cellulitis, on almost entirely non-randomised evidence.**
- **What happened:** A *Phlebology* systematic review (Nov 2025) searched five databases through June 2024 for cellulitis-prevention interventions other than antibiotic prophylaxis. Of 1,116 papers screened, 25 met criteria and **only one was a randomised controlled trial**. Nearly all addressed lymphoedema as the dominant modifiable risk factor, and its management — particularly compression — was consistently associated with reduced recurrence. No studies were found linking management of other guideline-listed risk factors (tinea, ulcers) to cellulitis recurrence.
- **Why people may care:** Cellulitis rate is a patient-important endpoint, near the top of this project's endpoint hierarchy and far above limb volume. This is the closest thing to an evidence summary supporting compression for infection prevention, and it supports framing compression adherence to patients as infection prevention rather than only volume control.
- **What limits confidence:** A systematic review inherits the design quality of its inputs, and 24 of 25 inputs were non-randomised — this is a well-organised summary of weak evidence, not strong evidence. The included studies are not stratified by etiology, so no primary-lymphedema-specific effect size can be extracted. The absence of evidence for tinea/ulcer management is an evidence gap, not a demonstration that treating them does not help.
- **Classification:** practice-relevant
- **Scores:** Importance 6/10 | Evidence 4/10 | Novelty 4/10 | Decision 6/10 | Calibration 8/10

**Somatic genotyping of complex lymphatic anomalies is broadening beyond PIK3CA/KRAS — 2 papers.** *Human Molecular Genetics (Jul 2026)* reports an NRAS p.Q61R mutation in a central conducting lymphatic anomaly and a PPFIBP1::ROS1 fusion in a Gorham-Stout disease patient, each a first report in its subtype. *Lymphatic Research and Biology* genotyped affected tissue from 58 vascular-anomaly patients on a custom 735-gene panel, finding pathogenic/likely-pathogenic variants in 47% overall and **61.5% of lymphatic malformations**. **KIF11-related MCLMR — 2 case reports, lymphedema absent in one.** A 39-year-old man with a novel likely-pathogenic frameshift (c.535del) had microcephaly and chorioretinal atrophy but **no lymphedema**; a 42-year-old woman with a de novo in-frame deletion (p.Glu432del) was followed over 40 years with severe intellectual disability, microcephaly, seizures and self-injurious behaviour. New finding `finding_kif11_mclmr_expressivity_2026`. Hypothesis-generating · Importance 4 · Evidence 2-4 · Novelty 5-6 · Decision 2 · Calibration 9. *Limits: n=1 each; variable expressivity in KIF11-MCLMR is long-established prior art, so these add catalog entries and follow-up duration, not a concept.*

**Novel PROX1 variant in a neonate with hydrops, chylothorax and chylous ascites** — *Frontiers in Cardiovascular Medicine, Jul 2026.* Newly surfaced by this run's own PubMed sweep. Heterozygous c.502C>T p.(Arg168Trp); the child died at ~7 months. New finding `finding_prox1_human_variant_hydrops_2026`. Hypothesis-generating · Importance 4 · Evidence 2 · Novelty 7 · Decision 2 · Calibration 7. *Limits: n=1, no functional validation, no segregation; pathogenicity rests on rarity plus phenotype plausibility. The outcome in this single case must not be presented to families as the expected course of a PROX1 variant.*

**CXCR4 implicated in lymphatic valve formation from two directions — 2 papers.** A bioRxiv preprint reports CXCL12/CXCR4 governing valve formation through flow-dependent AKT/FOXO1/**FOXC2** activation (a second upstream route converging on the established lymphedema-distichiasis gene). One source is a non-peer-reviewed preprint. Both are mouse-only.*

**AQP1 sustains lymphangiogenesis under inflammatory hyperosmotic stress** — *Journal of Experimental Medicine, Jul 2026.* Defines an inflammation-specific lymphangiogenic programme distinct from the embryonic one; AQP1 upregulated in human lymphedema and lymphatic malformation tissue. New finding `finding_aqp1_inflammatory_lymphangiogenesis_2026`. Hypothesis-generating · Importance 6 · Evidence 5 · Novelty 8 · Decision 2 · Calibration 8. *Limits: human tissue appears only as expression validation, not a patient-level result. AQP1 being elevated in lymphedematous tissue is equally consistent with it being a consequence of the disease as a contributor. Most primary lymphedema is developmental failure, so an inflammation-specific programme is more plausibly downstream.*

**Serum ICG quantification as a lymphatic function biomarker** — *Scientific Reports, Jul 2026.* Serum concentration after intradermal ICG fell across four preclinical models of lymphatic dysfunction (VEGFR3 haploinsufficiency, inducible LEC depletion, aging, surgical injury), correlating with vessel density, pumping and dendritic cell migration. Hypothesis-generating · Importance 5 · Evidence 3 · Novelty 8 · Decision 3 · Calibration 8. *Limits: entirely animal models; the paper's own framing is secondary lymphedema; no human validation, so this is a concept, not an assay.*

### Disease Management

**Non-antibiotic cellulitis prevention — systematic review** — See Highlight #3. New finding `finding_cellulitis_prevention_lymphoedema_management_2026`. Practice-relevant · Importance 6 · Evidence 4 · Novelty 4 · Decision 6 · Calibration 8.

**Only 7 of 145 contemporary lymphedema RCTs address pneumatic compression in the lower limb** — *J Vasc Surg Venous Lymphat Disord, Apr 2026.* A guideline-directed systematic review of IPC RCTs (2010-2025) found 21 upper-extremity RCTs versus 7 lower-extremity. New finding `finding_ipc_lower_extremity_rct_scarcity_2026`; also logged against `finding_surgical_evidence_gap_global`. Background/context · Importance 6 · Evidence 5 · Novelty 4 · Decision 5 · Calibration 8. *Limits: this finding records a counting claim about the literature, not an efficacy conclusion about IPC. Directly relevant to primary lymphedema, which is predominantly lower-limb: recommendations for IPC in this population are substantially extrapolated from upper-limb, largely breast-cancer-related cohorts. That is a reason for calibrated expectations, not for withholding a low-risk therapy.*

**Fetal lymphatic malformation volume ratio and delivery outcomes (n=18)** — *Prenatal Diagnosis, Jul 2026.* All cases with LVR > 5 cm² required post-natal tracheostomy; all with LVR > 4 cm² had delivery complications; intralesional bleeding (n=3) associated with LVR > 11 cm² and faster growth (2.6 vs 0.3 cm²/week, p=0.005). New finding `finding_fetal_lm_volume_ratio_prognostication_2026`. Hypothesis-generating · Importance 4 · Evidence 3 · Novelty 8 · Decision 4 · Calibration 8. *Limits: thresholds are derived and tested in the same 18-fetus dataset with no external validation — the classic setting for optimistic cut-points. Three bleeding events underpin one of the thresholds. The authors themselves call the observations exploratory, and this summary does not go beyond them.*

**Pediatric lymphatic malformations, n=478 over a decade** — *BMC Pediatrics, Jul 2026.* Beijing Children's Hospital, 2014-2024. Head and neck most involved (45.0%); cyst morphology showed distinct anatomic predilections (p<0.01); macrocystic lesions responded best across all modalities (p<0.05); **no significant difference in effective rate between surgery and sclerotherapy**. New finding `finding_pediatric_lm_modality_outcomes_2026`. Hypothesis-generating · Importance 5 · Evidence 4 · Novelty 7 · Decision 4 · Calibration 7. *Limits: treatment was allocated by clinical judgement, not randomised, so the surgery-versus-sclerotherapy equivalence is confounded by indication and is not a demonstration of equal efficacy — easier lesions may have gone to one arm. "Effective rate" is not a standardised outcome.*

**Sex, age and BMI as predictors of LVA/VLNT outcome — multicentre cohort** — *J Plast Reconstr Aesthet Surg, Aug 2026.* Male sex associated with greater response in lower-limb LVA (12, 24 mo) and VLNT (12 mo); higher BMI with worse response after upper-limb VLNT (12, 18 mo); no consistent age effect; no effect on complication rates. New finding `finding_lva_vlnt_patient_factor_predictors_2026`. Hypothesis-generating · Importance 5 · Evidence 4 · Novelty 8 · Decision 4 · Calibration 8. *Limits: retrospective, no control arm, volume-based surrogate endpoints. Associations are scattered across sex × limb × timepoint strata and significant in some but not others — a pattern consistent with multiple comparisons, with no correction described. **Obesity confounding is central here, not incidental**: BMI is simultaneously the exposure and a known independent driver of limb volume. The primary-lymphedema subgroup is not reported separately.*

**Ultrasound prediction of subcutaneous fibrosis (92 sites, 40 limbs, 26 patients)** — *Lymphatic Research and Biology, Aug 2026.* Skin thickness and fat echogenicity predicted intraoperatively-graded fibrosis severity (AUC 0.80 for calf, cutoff 2.65 mm; regression R²=0.51). Hypothesis-generating · Importance 3 · Evidence 3 · Novelty 5 · Decision 3 · Calibration 8. *Limits: retrospective, 26 patients (all female), single-operator 18-MHz technique, reference standard is subjective intraoperative grading by the same team, and the population is an LVA surgical cohort of unstated etiology. The paper's conclusion that these measures "improve surgical outcomes" is not supported — no outcome was measured.*

**Tendon and plantar fascia changes in chronic lower-limb edema (53 cases vs 55 controls)** — *Am J Phys Med Rehabil, Aug 2026.* Achilles tendon and plantar fascia thickening remained significant after BMI adjustment and correlated with edema volume; plantar fasciitis in 50.9% and Achilles tendinopathy in 22.6% of the edema group; worse LEFS, LYMQOL-Leg and VAS scores. Hypothesis-generating · Importance 4 · Evidence 5 · Novelty 6 · Decision 3 · Calibration 8. *Limits: cross-sectional with a real control group and BMI adjustment (a step above most of this literature), but causal direction is unresolved — edema may cause the tendon changes, or shared mechanical loading may cause both. Etiology of the edema cohort is not stated.*

**Case-level lymphatic malformation reports — 3 papers.** A gluteal macrocystic lymphatic malformation superinfected after minor trauma in a 2-year-old (*J Ultrason*); nine pediatric omental cysts over a decade (*J Indian Assoc Pediatr Surg*); giant scrotal elephantiasis with generalized lymphedema and sepsis, initially mistaken for a soft-tissue malignancy (*Aktuelle Urologie*). Hypothesis-generating / tracker maintenance · Importance 1-2 · Evidence 1-2 · Novelty 3-6 · Decision 1-2 · Calibration 7-8. *Limits: all single cases or tiny uncontrolled series describing recognised complications; none changes practice. The scrotal case does not specify the etiology of the underlying generalized lymphedema.*

## Negative / Null Results This Week

Two items, both worth more attention than their study designs would normally earn:

1. **Trametinib non-response in a RAS-pathway CCLA patient** (Highlight #2). This is the first documented targeted-therapy failure to enter `finding_mtor_mek_neonatal_lymphatic_anomalies_2026`, a finding otherwise built entirely from published successes. It is n=1 and proves nothing about trametinib's efficacy — but the asymmetry matters: successful n=1 targeted-therapy cases get published, failures usually do not. This finding's confidence was deliberately **not** upgraded this cycle despite a new supportive sirolimus report, specifically because of this case.

2. **No difference between surgery and sclerotherapy for pediatric lymphatic malformations** (n=478). A null comparative result, but a weak one — non-randomised allocation means indication confounding could hide a real difference in either direction. It is recorded as "no difference detected in a confounded comparison," not as equivalence.

Additionally, the **absence** of any study linking tinea or ulcer management to cellulitis recurrence (Highlight #3) is an evidence gap being reported as such, and should not be read as evidence those interventions do not work.

## Evolving Understanding

- **Primary lymphedema genetics is converging on valve mechanobiology.** PLXND1 as a mechanosensor within a "lymphatic mechanocomplex," CXCL12/CXCR4 → AKT/FOXO1/FOXC2, the previously-tracked LPA → Rho-kinase → NF-κB → FOXC2 axis, and PIEZO1 mechanosensing all describe the same problem: how lymphatic endothelium converts flow into the valve transcriptional programme. That convergence is real and is the most coherent organising theme in this knowledge base's genetics dimension. What it is **not** is progress toward treatment — every one of these is mouse or preprint work, and the field's preclinical-to-clinical translation record in lymphedema remains poor. The honest summary is that the map is improving while the therapeutic distance is unchanged.

- **Targeted therapy for complex lymphatic anomalies: the picture got more complicated, not better.** This cycle added a possible new gene (SHOC2), a new somatic driver class (NRAS, a ROS1 fusion), a preclinical argument for inhibiting RAS upstream rather than MEK downstream, evidence that PI3Kα is engaged alongside MAPK — and a clinical trametinib failure in a RAS-pathway patient whose mechanism turned out to look non-canonical. Taken together these argue that pathway-level genotyping is not sufficient to predict drug response in CCLA, and that mechanism-level characterisation should precede committing a critically ill patient to a targeted agent. `finding_mtor_mek_neonatal_lymphatic_anomalies_2026` stays at `low_to_moderate`; the deliberate decision not to upgrade it despite a new positive report is documented in its evolution log.

- **Somatic tissue sequencing has a defensible diagnostic yield.** 61.5% pathogenic/likely-pathogenic detection in lymphatic malformation tissue is the most decision-useful number in the genetics dimension this week, because it supports a concrete pathway choice — biopsy the lesion rather than rely on germline testing. New finding at `low_to_moderate`; the constraint is that it comes from one single-centre cohort on a custom panel, so the number should not be quoted as generalisable.

- **The lower-limb evidence deficit is now quantified.** 7 lower-extremity versus 21 upper-extremity IPC RCTs out of 145. Primary lymphedema is predominantly a lower-limb disease, so this is not an abstract asymmetry — it means the randomised base for a commonly recommended therapy sits in a population primary-lymphedema patients do not resemble. This sharpens rather than changes `finding_surgical_evidence_gap_global` (unchanged at `high`).

- **Cellulitis prevention is where the patient-important evidence is, and it is thin.** Compression's association with reduced cellulitis recurrence rests on 24 non-randomised studies and one RCT. That it is the best-supported patient-important outcome in the field says as much about the field as about compression.

- **No finding had its confidence downgraded this cycle.** Nothing published in the window contradicted an existing finding. Two upgrades were considered and declined: `finding_mtor_mek_neonatal_lymphatic_anomalies_2026` (for the reason above) and `finding_lpa_foxc2_axis_2026` (new convergent evidence, but from a preprint).

## Skepticism Notes

- **The ultrasound-fibrosis paper overclaims in its conclusion.** It states that the findings "support the use of ultrasound imaging to guide treatment planning and improve surgical outcomes." No surgical outcome was measured in the study. The measurable claim — that skin thickness and fat echogenicity correlate with intraoperatively-graded fibrosis (AUC 0.80, R²=0.51) — is reasonable; the outcome claim is not supported by the design.

- **Multiple-comparison risk in the LVA/VLNT predictor cohort.** Effects appear for male sex in the lower limb at 12 and 24 months but not consistently elsewhere, and for BMI in the upper limb at 12 and 18 months but not the lower. Testing three variables across two limbs, two operations and several timepoints generates many comparisons; no correction is described. Treat these as hypotheses.

- **BMI is not a nuisance variable in that study — it is the exposure.** Because weight loss alone reduces limb volume and the outcome is volume-based, a BMI-outcome association is partly definitional. The standing obesity-confounding caution applies with unusual force here.

- **A paper was incorporated whose methods cannot be read.** The *J Perinatol* sirolimus NICU report has no abstract on PubMed. It is recorded as a citation against an existing finding, not as evidence, and contributed nothing to any confidence level. Flagging this so it is not later mistaken for assessable data.

- **One source this cycle is a preprint.** The CXCL12/CXCR4 valve paper is bioRxiv, not peer-reviewed. It is deliberately grouped with the VHL/CXCR4 paper rather than given its own finding, and the two point in opposite directions on whether CXCR4 signalling should be raised or lowered.

- **Stale-item accounting.** Most of what was synthesized this week is **not** new in the W33 window. The PLXND1, SHOC2, cellulitis-prevention, IPC-review, fetal-LM and pediatric-LM papers were published between November 2025 and July 2026 and have been in this project's database since 2026-07-21 or earlier. They are new *to this knowledge base*, not new *to the field*. The genuinely-new-this-window items are four low-value scan additions plus four papers found by this run's own sweep. The publication event and the discovery event are not the same thing, and this week the gap between them was 26 days for the most important item.

- **The backlog is a process risk, not a one-off.** A status-vocabulary mismatch between the scan writer and the deep reader silently hid 33 papers from two consecutive syntheses. Nothing errored. The reason it was caught is that this run enumerated papers by `added_date` rather than trusting the status filter. Until the selection logic is fixed, the same class of failure can recur with any status value a future scan writer invents.
