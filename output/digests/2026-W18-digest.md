## Highlights

### Highlight 1: LPA → LPA4/LPA6 → ROCK → NF-κB → FOXC2 — A Druggable Upstream Pathway in Lymphedema-Distichiasis Biology

- **What happened:** Yasuda et al. (*Journal of Clinical Investigation*, January 13, 2026; alerted by 2026-04-23 scan) showed that LEC-specific deletion of two GPCR receptors for lysophosphatidic acid (LPA4 and LPA6) in mice phenocopies LEC-specific Foxc2 deletion, with impaired lymphatic valve formation and maintenance. The signaling axis runs LPA → LPA4/LPA6 → Gα12/Gα13 → Rho kinase → NF-κB → FOXC2. Pharmacologic inhibition of NF-κB or Rho kinase impaired valve maintenance in the same model.
- **Why people may care:** This is the first description of an extracellular regulatory layer above FOXC2 (previously, only oscillatory shear stress was known to induce FOXC2). The pathway nominates several druggable nodes — Rho-kinase, NF-κB, LPA receptors — that could in principle be tested for the ability to rescue valve function in hypomorphic FOXC2 / lymphedema-distichiasis (LDS) settings.
- **What limits confidence:** Mouse model only; no human validation; no patient-derived cell or organoid data; no demonstration that pharmacologic agents rescue an LDS-like phenotype. Lymphedema preclinical-to-clinical translation has a poor track record (preclinical translation warning per framework). Druggable-node identification is not the same as a viable drug program.
- **Classification:** hypothesis-generating
- **Scores:** Importance 7/10 | Evidence 5/10 | Novelty 8/10 | Decision 3/10 | Calibration 9/10

### Highlight 2: First Multi-Population GWAS Dedicated to Lymphedema and CVI (Million Veteran Program)

- **What happened:** Peloso et al. (*Circulation: Genomic and Precision Medicine*, April 22, 2026; alerted by 2026-04-23 scan) reported the first multi-population GWAS dedicated to lymphedema and chronic venous insufficiency (CVI), conducted in the Million Veteran Program with cancer cases excluded (lymphedema-only n=2,452; lymphedema+CVI n=3,283; CVI-only n=34,664; controls n=367,684). Eleven genome-wide significant variants were identified — 8 CVI-only, 1 lymphedema-only, 2 lymphedema+CVI — plus 2 population-specific variants. Three CVI-only loci (CASZ1, SLC12A2, NDP) replicated in UK Biobank. Polygenic risk scores derived from MVP were associated with CVI in UKB, and CVI-associated variants showed pleiotropy with cardiometabolic traits.
- **Why people may care:** Provides the first map of common-variant genetic architecture for non-cancer lymphedema; bridges Mendelian primary-lymphedema gene catalogues (FLT4, FOXC2, GJC2, PIEZO1, etc.) with a population-scale common-variant signal. The cancer-excluded cohort design reduces (though does not eliminate) BCRL contamination relative to most prior lymphedema EHR samples.
- **What limits confidence:** EHR-based phecode ascertainment for lymphedema captures a heterogeneous population (primary, idiopathic, post-surgical-non-cancer, post-infectious). The lymphedema-only PRS was not replicated in UKB — only the CVI PRS was. Specific lymphedema-only and lymphedema+CVI loci have not yet been fine-mapped or replicated in an independent lymphedema cohort. Not actionable for primary-lymphedema risk stratification or counseling at this time.
- **Classification:** hypothesis-generating (toward pipeline-relevant once lymphedema loci are fine-mapped)
- **Scores:** Importance 7/10 | Evidence 6/10 | Novelty 7/10 | Decision 4/10 | Calibration 8/10

### Highlight 3: GATA2 Deficiency Case Series Reinforces Existing Diagnostic Imperative — Not a Practice Change

- **What happened:** Dong et al. (*Journal of Clinical Immunology*, March 23, 2026) reported three sporadic cases of GATA2 deficiency syndrome (germline variants c.818dupG and c.1084C>T) presenting with combinations of primary lymphedema, cytopenias, recurrent pulmonary infection, and congenital sensorineural deafness, alongside a literature review of the broader phenotype.
- **Why people may care:** GATA2 deficiency is the single most clinically actionable primary-lymphedema diagnosis because of MDS/AML risk and immunologic complications. Each well-characterized case series reinforces the threshold for testing GATA2 in primary lymphedema with cytopenia, deafness, or atypical infections.
- **What limits confidence:** Three-patient retrospective case series (n=3); no new mechanism, variant class, or treatment data. Confirmatory only. Not an evidence elevation for any clinical recommendation that did not already exist.
- **Classification:** background/context (with practice-reinforcing diagnostic message)
- **Scores:** Importance 6/10 | Evidence 3/10 | Novelty 4/10 | Decision 5/10 | Calibration 8/10

## Evolving Understanding

### Primary lymphedema genetics — the upstream regulatory landscape of FOXC2 expands

The Yasuda LPA→FOXC2 paper materially extends what is known about FOXC2 regulation in lymphatic-distichiasis biology. Until now, only oscillatory shear stress was a recognised extracellular FOXC2 inducer. The LPA4/LPA6 GPCR axis operating through Rho-kinase and NF-κB places FOXC2 within a much more druggable signaling environment. *Confidence change:* a new finding `finding_lpa_foxc2_axis_2026` is added at low confidence (mouse-only, preclinical). The companion piece is mechanistic, not therapeutic — none of this is clinically actionable for LDS patients today.

### Primary lymphedema genetics — common-variant architecture starts to fill in

The MVP GWAS is the first dedicated, multi-population, cancer-excluded common-variant study of lymphedema and CVI. The lymphedema-specific signal is real but small (1 lymphedema-only and 2 shared lymphedema+CVI loci) and the lymphedema-only PRS did not replicate in UKB. The CVI side of the study is more robust. Nevertheless, the work begins to outline a connection between the established Mendelian genes (FLT4, FOXC2, GJC2, PIEZO1) and the broader population-genetic landscape of lymphatic dysfunction. A new finding `finding_mvp_gwas_lymphedema_cvi_2026` is added at low_to_moderate confidence. Practical takeaway for clinicians and patients: this is **not** a polygenic-risk-score deployment story yet.

### Primary lymphedema genetics — mouse mechanism papers continue to outpace human translation

Five mouse mechanistic / cell-biology papers entered the database across W17–W18 (Yasuda LPA-FOXC2; Shoc2-mTORC1-IFN; NRAS Q61R KLA model; endothelial VHL/HIF/CXCR4; endothelial RhoA-KLF2-Notch1). Each is well-designed within its scope. None has human variant or clinical-trial linkage. Together they reinforce how productive lymphatic developmental biology remains as a discipline — and how fragile the bridge from mouse mechanism to LDS / Milroy / Hennekam patient care continues to be. Confidence on `finding_piezo1_pathway_preclinical` and the broader preclinical translation caveat remains unchanged.

### Pediatric complex lymphatic anomalies — imaging-led, genotype-informed practice is now the consensus

The Klein *Pediatric Radiology* review and the Sharma *Annals of Plastic Surgery* case series both describe the same operating model: intranodal-contrast-enhanced MR lymphangiography for phenotype, genetic testing to identify driver mutations (PROS/PIK3CA, RASopathy, GLA), and stratified systemic therapy (sirolimus, alpelisib, propranolol, corticosteroids) integrated with localized treatments and supportive care. This is consolidation of practice — not an evidence elevation. No new finding is created; the existing `finding_piezo1_pathway_preclinical` and the broader pediatric/CLA framework in KB05 are preserved with a small update for completeness.

### GATA2 deficiency — diagnostic message reinforced, no new evidence layer

The Dong case series re-affirms that any primary lymphedema patient with cytopenia, sensorineural deafness, or unusual infections deserves GATA2 testing. KB03 already states this. No finding update.

### Imaging — methodologic refinements only

Two imaging items in this cycle (dual-injection lymphoscintigraphy abstract; neonatal pulmonary lymphatic MR case) are small additions. The Zhao *Acta Physiologica Sinica* methods review is helpful as a cross-modality reference but does not specify primary-lymphedema-specific protocols. The Klein *Pediatric Radiology* review consolidates the imaging-led pediatric framework. None of these revises `finding_mri_central_lymphatics_primary_2026`, which retains low_to_moderate confidence pending outcome-linked validation work.

## Skepticism Notes

1. **MVP GWAS — case ascertainment caveats.** EHR phecode-defined lymphedema in the VA system captures a heterogeneous mix of etiologies; primary-lymphedema cases are likely a small minority. The lymphedema PRS did not replicate in UKB. The "first GWAS for lymphedema" framing is correct but should not be inflated into a primary-lymphedema risk-prediction tool. Calibration retained at 8/10.

2. **LPA-FOXC2 mouse mechanism — druggable ≠ translatable.** Nominating Rho-kinase or NF-κB as druggable nodes is biologically reasonable, but lymphedema preclinical-to-clinical translation has been poor (Lymfactin Phase II missed primary endpoints; multiple PIEZO1 agonist programs have not entered humans). "Druggable" is a 5-year hypothesis statement, not a near-term clinical lead.

3. **GATA2 case series — n=3, no new mechanism.** Useful diagnostic reminder but not an evidence elevation. The 7/10 importance reflects the clinical stakes of missing GATA2, not the strength of this report.

4. **Pediatric CLA review — practice consensus, not evidence elevation.** Klein 2026 consolidates an imaging-and-genotype-led approach that is already standard at major centers. Cite as reference, not as a practice-change driver.

5. **OBSCN watch (carried forward from W16).** No independent replication of the GJC2+OBSCN co-segregating pedigree has appeared. OBSCN should not be added to diagnostic gene panels. Watchlisted.

6. **VEGFR3-HSPG2 preprint — abstract-only.** ss_3c57b92fa67d is a bioRxiv preprint without full methods. Mechanistic lead only; do not over-interpret without peer review.

7. **Lymphedema-as-cardiovascular-disorder review — educational, not new evidence.** ss_c1db89590550 is cross-disciplinary outreach for a cardiology audience. No new patient-management content.

8. **Surgical evidence gap — still no new RCT.** Across W17–W18 ingestion, no controlled trial of LVA, VLNT, ILR, or surgical embolization for primary lymphedema appeared. The pattern documented in `finding_surgical_evidence_gap_global` continues. The Symani trials (NCT06866197, NCT06725030) and the MSK ILR trial (NCT06144164) remain the most systematic active prospective studies, but all are currently RECRUITING.
