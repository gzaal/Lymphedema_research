## Highlights

### Highlight 1: Novel Biallelic FAT4 Splicing Variant Expands Hennekam Syndrome 2 Allelic Spectrum

- **What happened:** Mascarenhas & Gupta (*American Journal of Medical Genetics Part A*, April 16, 2026) reported a 15-month-old with peripheral lymphedema, dysmorphic facies, and camptodactyly harboring a novel biallelic splicing variant c.12479+3A>G in FAT4, the gene associated with Hennekam lymphangiectasia-lymphedema syndrome 2 (OMIM 616006).
- **Why people may care:** FAT4-associated Hennekam is under-reported relative to the CCBE1/ADAMTS3 forms; each credible novel variant adds to the curation record used by genetic-testing laboratories and ClinVar/LOVD submitters.
- **What limits confidence:** Single case report (n=1), no in vitro splicing assay or cDNA-level confirmation shown in the abstract, and no functional phenotyping. A splice-site prediction alone does not establish pathogenicity. This is a data point, not a practice change.
- **Classification:** hypothesis-generating
- **Scores:** Importance 4/10 | Evidence 2/10 | Novelty 9/10 | Decision 2/10 | Calibration 9/10

### Highlight 2: GJC2 Missense + OBSCN Co-segregating Variants in an AD Primary-Lymphedema Pedigree — Interpret OBSCN Cautiously

- **What happened:** Shi et al. (*Orphanet Journal of Rare Diseases*, January 13, 2026; newly indexed this cycle) described a four-generation Chinese pedigree with autosomal-dominant generalized primary lymphedema. Whole-exome sequencing plus Sanger validation in 26 relatives identified two novel heterozygous missense variants that co-segregate with disease: GJC2 c.287G>T (p.Gly96Val) in the second transmembrane domain of connexin 47 (an established lymphedema gene) and OBSCN c.22393T>A (p.Phe7465Ile), not previously implicated in lymphatic disease.
- **Why people may care:** If replicated, OBSCN would be a new candidate gene, and the GJC2 variant enriches the list of disease-associated connexin-47 alterations available for diagnostic interpretation.
- **What limits confidence:** Single-family report. OBSCN (obscurin) is a very large sarcomeric gene in which rare missense variants are common in the general population; single-pedigree co-segregation is not sufficient evidence of causation. No functional data (splicing, transmission electron microscopy, or lymphatic-endothelial assays) are reported. OBSCN should not be added to diagnostic panels or used in family counseling until independent replication appears.
- **Classification:** hypothesis-generating
- **Scores:** Importance 5/10 | Evidence 3/10 | Novelty 4/10 | Decision 2/10 | Calibration 8/10

### Highlight 3: Largest Primary-Lymphedema MRI Cohort to Date Correlates MRTD Features With ISL Stage

- **What happened:** Liu et al. (*Vascular Medicine*, February 1, 2026) reported MR thoracic ductography (MRTD) findings in 279 primary lower-extremity lymphedema patients stratified by ISL stage (I/II/III), proposing imaging correlates of clinical stage for the thoracic duct, cisterna chyli, and inguinal drainage.
- **Why people may care:** One of the largest imaging cohorts restricted to primary lymphedema ever published. Standardized central-lymphatic imaging is underdeveloped in this patient population and could eventually support better pre-operative characterization.
- **What limits confidence:** Cross-sectional design; surrogate endpoint (imaging grade correlated with clinical stage); no prospective outcome linkage, no inter-rater reproducibility reported, no treatment-response validation. Expected correlation between imaging and stage does not establish prognostic or treatment-predictive value.
- **Classification:** hypothesis-generating (toward practice-relevant with outcome linkage)
- **Scores:** Importance 5/10 | Evidence 5/10 | Novelty 3/10 | Decision 3/10 | Calibration 8/10

## Skepticism Notes

1. **OBSCN extraordinary claim.** Proposing a novel primary-lymphedema disease gene from a single family on the basis of co-segregation alone is an extraordinary claim. Large genes produce frequent background variants; a single pedigree cannot distinguish causation from linkage with a true causal variant. The GJC2 variant in the same pedigree is the more credible cause. Treat the "co-segregating OBSCN variant" framing as hypothesis-generating only.

2. **Single-arm surgical-volume claims persist.** The duo-plane LVB and super-microsurgical LVA-by-Yamamoto reports illustrate the chronic surgical-evidence gap. Neither has a comparator, standardized outcome measures, or obesity control. Pooled single-arm data cannot substitute for a control group.

3. **MRTD correlation with stage is not a treatment-prediction claim.** The Liu 2026 paper reports imaging features that correlate with ISL stage — an expected finding. Without prospective outcome linkage or inter-rater reproducibility data, MRTD is not yet a pre-operative decision tool for primary lymphedema.

4. **IPC-device systematic review is not primary-specific.** O'Donnell & Tedesco 2026 aggregates heterogeneous RCTs across mixed lymphedema populations with surrogate endpoints. Its relevance to primary-lymphedema management should not be over-generalized.

5. **Automated relevance scoring remains conservative.** The scan ingested 17 in-scope papers but assigned no paper relevance ≥7 — hence no alert. This is consistent with a genuinely quiet primary-lymphedema publication week, but the FAT4 Hennekam report (newly published, primary-typed, novel variant) arguably merits relevance 6 rather than 5. Consider revisiting scan scoring heuristics for `rare-syndrome + novel-variant + first-report` combinations.
