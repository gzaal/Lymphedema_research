## Highlights

### Highlight 1: RAS-MAPK Pathway Variants in Non-Syndromic Primary Lymphedema — First Report

- **What happened:** Belanova et al. (*Lymphology*, August 2025; PMID 40803040) retrospectively analyzed 408 Italian primary lymphedema patients and identified five variants in **LZTR1**, **MAP2K1**, and **RAF1** — four previously unreported. In silico molecular modeling supported potential pathogenicity of a RAF1 variant. Crucially, these patients did not have a Noonan syndrome diagnosis; the variants appeared in clinically non-syndromic primary lymphedema. A PubMed search returns zero prior publications linking LZTR1 to lymphedema in any context. RAF1 and MAP2K1 have previously been reported in lymphedema only within the Noonan/RASopathy syndrome framework.

- **Why people may care:** The RAS/MAPK pathway is already known to be disrupted in **syndromic** primary lymphedema (Noonan syndrome: PTPN11, SOS1, RAF1, MAP2K1, etc.), and somatic RAS/MAPK mutations drive complex lymphatic anomalies (KRAS, BRAF). If germline RAS/MAPK variants below the threshold for full Noonan syndrome phenotype can nonetheless produce isolated lymphedema, it would blur the diagnostic boundary between syndromic and non-syndromic disease. This has implications for genetic panel design and counseling: clinicians encountering unsolved primary lymphedema with RAS/MAPK variants of uncertain significance may now have supporting (though weak) literature.

- **What limits confidence:** Retrospective design; functional validation completely absent — pathogenicity is computational only; single Italian cohort with potential population-specific founder effects; extremely low allele count (5 variants in 408 patients); no clinical comparison of RAS/MAPK-positive vs RAS/MAPK-negative primary lymphedema patients. Independent replication in a second cohort is required before these genes are added to diagnostic panels.

- **Classification:** hypothesis-generating
- **Scores:** Importance 6/10 | Evidence 4/10 | Novelty 7/10 | Decision 3/10 | Calibration 8/10

### Highlight 2: VLNT May Outperform LVA in Primary Lymphedema — Hypothesis-Generating Retrospective

- **What happened:** Lindenblatt et al. (*Journal of Surgical Research*, November 2025) retrospectively analyzed 48 limbs (mixed primary and secondary lymphedema) undergoing VLNT or LVA. In primary lymphedema patients specifically, **VLNT achieved significantly greater limb volume reduction than LVA alone**. In secondary lymphedema patients, no significant difference between modalities was found.

- **Why people may care:** This is one of very few publications that **separately analyses surgical outcomes in primary vs. secondary lymphedema patients**. If VLNT is specifically superior in primary lymphedema (possibly because the underlying developmental defect responds better to lymph-node transplantation than to anastomosis of incompletely developed lymphatic vessels), this would inform surgical planning and patient selection discussions. Primary lymphedema patients are underrepresented in most surgical series.

- **What limits confidence:** 48 limbs is a very small dataset; retrospective design; volume reduction is a surrogate endpoint — no patient-important outcomes (cellulitis rate, QoL, function) are reported; obesity confounding not controlled (surgical evidence caution: weight loss alone reduces lymphedema volume); no standardized patient-selection criteria reported; primary/secondary subgroup is even smaller. The claim is flagged as overstated relative to the evidence ("headline vs evidence: overstated" in metadata). **LVA/VLNT studies universally lack RCT-level evidence — surgical evidence gap flag applies.**

- **Classification:** hypothesis-generating
- **Scores:** Importance 6/10 | Evidence 4/10 | Novelty 5/10 | Decision 5/10 | Calibration 7/10

### Highlight 3: Lymphatic Embolization for Primary Genital Lymphorrhea — Early Evidence for a Difficult Problem

- **What happened:** Moon et al. (*Journal of Vascular and Interventional Radiology*, January 2025) reported a retrospective series of **11 patients** with primary genital lymphorrhea (lymph leakage) and chylous-reflux-related lower limb lymphedema treated with lymphatic embolization. Clinical success was **90% for genital lymphorrhea** but only **50% for associated leg lymphedema**. Technical success (embolization completion) was 100%.

- **Why people may care:** Genital lymphorrhea in primary lymphedema is a difficult complication — disfiguring, functionally limiting, and with limited documented treatment options. A 90% clinical success rate for the genital component represents a meaningful early signal for a minimally invasive approach. The 50% leg lymphedema response is less impressive and limits the overall claim.

- **What limits confidence:** 11 patients; single-arm retrospective; no comparison to surgical alternatives; varied follow-up durations; the mixed genital vs. limb response rate limits generalizable claims; underlying etiology of "primary" genital lymphorrhea is heterogeneous. The headline overpromises relative to the evidence.

- **Classification:** hypothesis-generating
- **Scores:** Importance 6/10 | Evidence 4/10 | Novelty 6/10 | Decision 5/10 | Calibration 7/10

## Evolving Understanding

### The genetic architecture of non-syndromic primary lymphedema is broader than Mendelian models predict

The two Kozáčiková/Belanova papers (same Italian cohort, n=408) are part of a multi-year systematic gene-discovery effort from this group. Looking across their publications since 2018:

- **2018**: 10-gene NGS panel in 116 patients — FLT4, FOXC2, GJC2 most common; MET/HGF variants identified
- **2020**: TIE1 novel candidates (n=235 negative for known genes)
- **2020**: ARAP3 novel candidates
- **2020**: Two rare PROX1 variants
- **2025**: HGF-MET dedicated deep-dive (n=408)
- **2025**: RAS-MAPK (LZTR1, MAP2K1, RAF1) in non-syndromic primary LE (n=408)

This work consistently demonstrates that **many different pathways** can produce a primary lymphedema phenotype. The field's diagnostic gap (>60% of familial cases unexplained) reflects genuine genetic heterogeneity, not just incomplete panels. The emerging picture:

1. The VEGF-C/VEGFR-3/PROX1/FOXC2 axis remains the dominant molecular highway.
2. Secondary pathways (PIEZO1 mechanosensory, Angiopoietin-TIE, HGF-MET, RAS/MAPK) are present at low prevalence (~1-3% each) and may require whole-exome or genome-scale approaches rather than targeted panel testing to identify.
3. The boundary between syndromic and non-syndromic primary lymphedema may be less absolute than previously modeled: the Belanova 2025 RAS/MAPK findings suggest pathway activation below the Noonan syndrome clinical threshold can still disrupt lymphatic development.

**This does not change the first-tier diagnostic gene panel recommendation** (FLT4, FOXC2, GJC2, PROX1, GATA2, PIEZO1, CCBE1, ADAMTS3, KIF11 + others in academic panels). It does reinforce the case for whole-exome sequencing in panel-negative primary lymphedema patients.

### Surgical evidence gap: primary lymphedema still poorly represented

The Lindenblatt 2025 paper stands out for directly comparing primary vs. secondary lymphedema surgical outcomes — a rare design feature. The finding that VLNT may be specifically superior in primary lymphedema is plausible (primary lymphedema has developmental lymphatic vessel abnormalities that may not support LVA as well as post-surgical disruption does), but the evidence is far too limited to guide practice. The field continues to lack RCTs for surgical intervention.

## Skepticism Notes

1. **Lindenblatt 2025 (VLNT vs LVA)**: The study's own metadata flags "headline vs evidence: overstated." The claim of VLNT superiority in primary lymphedema is directionally interesting but made from 48 total limbs split between two procedures and two disease categories — the relevant primary-lymphedema-VLNT subgroup is likely 10-15 limbs. Obesity confounding is not controlled. Do not use this to counsel patients on surgical modality preference.

2. **Moon 2025 (lymphatic embolization)**: 90% clinical success headline for genital lymphorrhea is derived from n=11. Technical success (embolization completed) does not equal clinical success (symptom resolution). The 50% leg lymphedema response rate is buried and receives less prominence than the 90% genital figure. Both figures are from a single-arm retrospective series with no comparison group.

3. **Both Italian cohort genetics papers**: Computational pathogenicity prediction (molecular modeling, in silico tools) is not equivalent to functional validation. The field has seen multiple computational predictions later disproved by wet-lab assays. Until in vitro or in vivo functional data are available for these variants, classification as "likely pathogenic" should be considered provisional.

4. **FLT4 congenital fetal series** (Zhai 2025, n=3): Variable postnatal courses ranging from spontaneous regression to surgical intervention in only 3 cases is not sufficient to make prognostic statements about FLT4-positive prenatally diagnosed lymphedema. Counseling families requires acknowledgment of substantial uncertainty.
