## ALERT — Hypothesis-generating — PLXND1 identified as a lymphatic mechanosensor required for valve morphogenesis, with functionally validated variants in primary lymphedema patients

**What happened:** A mouse genetics and cell-biology study identified the semaphorin receptor plexin D1 (PLXND1) as a lymphatic endothelial mechanosensor required for lymphatic valve morphogenesis, and reported PLXND1 missense variants in a screen of patients with primary lymphedema, two of which were functionally characterised as pathogenic — one disrupting ligand binding and one disrupting mechanosensing.

**Why people may care:** Lymphatic valve failure is a core mechanism across several primary lymphedema genotypes, and a mechanosensing pathway with matching human variants nominates PLXND1 as a candidate gene worth considering for primary-lymphedema sequencing panels and as a mechanistic target; variants in other members of the same mechanocomplex also disrupted complex formation, suggesting a pathway rather than a single gene.

**What limits confidence:** The causal evidence is preclinical — conditional mouse knockouts and in vitro assays — while the human component is variant-level functional work reported without cohort frequencies, family segregation, or phenotype detail, and lymphatic biology has a poor preclinical-to-clinical translation record, so no therapeutic or diagnostic inference is justified yet.

**Classification:** hypothesis-generating

**Study design:** preclinical — conditional lymphatic-endothelial-specific gene ablation in two vascular beds, mechanocomplex biochemistry, plus functional characterisation of patient-derived missense variants
**Publication event:** full-paper-published
**Endpoint:** exploratory — lymphatic valve morphogenesis, transcriptional program activation, variant function
**Comparator:** active (conditional knockout vs littermate control; wild-type vs variant protein)
**Result:** positive
**Skepticism flags:** Preclinical animal model with limited translational validity in lymphedema; human genetic evidence is variant-level only, without reported cohort size, allele frequency, or segregation; narrow population

## Below threshold — noted, not alerted

These did not meet the alert bar (importance ≥ 7 **and** evidence ≥ 5) but are the items most
worth a reader's attention this cycle.

- **SHOC2 as a novel cause of central conducting lymphatic anomaly** (pm_42454412, *Am J Med Genet A*, importance 6 / evidence 4). n=1 variant of uncertain significance with zebrafish functional support. The notable element is **negative**: trametinib produced no clinical improvement, and immunoblotting pointed to non-canonical mTOR rather than RAS-MAPK signalling. This is a useful caution against assuming MEK-inhibitor benefit in RASopathy-adjacent CCLA. Extends the existing `mtor_mek_neonatal_lymphatic_anomalies` finding.

- **Intermittent pneumatic compression RCT systematic review** (pm_41997536, *J Vasc Surg Venous Lymphat Disord*, importance 6 / evidence 5 — the methodologically strongest item this cycle, but short of the importance bar). GRADE-assessed, guideline-feeding, and it documents a structural asymmetry: only 7 randomised trials exist for lower extremity lymphedema versus 21 for upper extremity. Lower extremity is where most primary lymphedema presents.

- **Secondary prevention of cellulitis** (pm_41275489, *Phlebology*, importance 6 / evidence 4). 25 studies, nearly all centred on lymphoedema management, and **only one randomised trial**. Cellulitis recurrence is among the few genuinely patient-important endpoints in primary lymphedema, and the evidence behind preventing it is thinner than the confidence with which it is advised. A related Australasian equipoise survey (pm_42475483) signals a randomised trial in preparation — **added to watchlist**.

- **Tissue sodium falls after CDT while limb circumference does not** (pm_41883042, *Lymphat Res Biol*, importance 5 / evidence 3). n=11, uncontrolled pre/post, so the treatment effect itself is not established — but the dissociation between a sodium readout that moved and a volume readout that did not (p = 0.764) is a relevant argument about endpoint selection in a field that defaults to volume. Follow-on to pm_42286847.

- **Primary-subgroup signal in VLNT recipient siting** (pm_42085896, *J Surg Res*, importance 5 / evidence 3). Multilevel groin-plus-ankle inset outperformed single-site inset, with the advantage reported as concentrated in the primary lymphedema subgroup. Surgical series that separate primary from secondary outcomes are rare enough to be worth noting — but this is retrospective, n=51, volume-only, 12 months, with no adjustment for weight change, and the subgroup interaction is exploratory.

   **The fix is already staged and unapplied** in `settings.local.FIXED.json`, and it is a
   two-line change:

   ```diff
   -  "Write(/Users/geertzaal/Developer/Lymphedema_research/*)",
   +  "Write(/Users/geertzaal/Developer/Lymphedema_research/**)",
   +  "Edit(/Users/geertzaal/Developer/Lymphedema_research/**)",
   ```

   I have deliberately **not** applied this — it widens my own write permissions, which is a
   change that should be yours to make. Until it is applied, every automated run will keep
