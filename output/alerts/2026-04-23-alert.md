## ALERT — Hypothesis-generating — Multi-ancestry GWAS in the Million Veteran Program maps new loci for lymphedema and chronic venous insufficiency

**What happened:** A multi-population genome-wide association study of 367,684 cancer-free Million Veteran Program controls vs 34,664 CVI-only, 2,452 lymphedema-only, and 3,283 lymphedema+CVI cases identified 11 genome-wide-significant variants (8 for CVI only, 1 for lymphedema only, 2 for lymphedema+CVI), with three CVI loci (near *CASZ1*, *SLC12A2*, *NDP*) replicating in the UK Biobank.

**Why people may care:** This is the first dedicated multi-ancestry GWAS to report a lymphedema-specific locus and shared lymphedema+CVI loci in a cancer-free cohort, extending the genetic map of lymphatic/venous dysfunction beyond the classical monogenic primary-lymphedema genes (FLT4, FOXC2, GATA2, PIEZO1, etc.) and providing a polygenic risk score that transferred to UK Biobank.

**What limits confidence:** The lymphedema phenotype is EHR-coded and mixes primary, obesity-associated, and non-cancer secondary lymphedema; only one lymphedema-only locus was found and lymphedema-specific replication was not demonstrated, so heterogeneity and residual confounding (especially obesity and CVI) cannot be excluded as drivers of signal.

**Classification:** hypothesis-generating
**Study design:** observational multi-ancestry GWAS with UK Biobank replication
**Publication event:** full-paper-published (Circulation: Genomic and Precision Medicine, 2026-04-22)
**Endpoint:** exploratory — locus discovery and PRS association
**Comparator:** 367,684 cancer-free controls; UK Biobank used as replication cohort
**Result:** positive (locus discovery with partial replication)
**Skepticism flags:** Mixed primary/secondary phenotype definition; lymphedema-only signal did not have dedicated replication shown.

## ALERT — Hypothesis-generating — LPA4/LPA6 → NF-κB → FOXC2 axis identified as an extracellular regulator of lymphatic valve development

**What happened:** Using lymphatic-endothelial-cell-specific conditional knockout mice, the authors show that combined LPA4 and LPA6 receptor loss impairs FOXC2 expression and lymphatic valve formation/maintenance, phenocopying LEC-specific *Foxc2* deletion; the signal runs through Gα12/Gα13 → Rho kinase → NF-κB, and pharmacologic NF-κB or Rho-kinase inhibition reproduced the valve-maintenance defect.

**Why people may care:** FOXC2 is the gene underlying lymphoedema-distichiasis syndrome (LDS), and until now the extracellular inputs that regulate FOXC2 expression (beyond oscillatory shear stress) were unclear. Identifying LPA/LPA4-LPA6/NF-κB as the upstream axis points to druggable nodes (GPCR agonism, NF-κB or ROCK modulation) for future therapeutic hypotheses in FOXC2-related primary lymphedema.

**What limits confidence:** Entirely mouse preclinical work with no human patient data or drug-like candidate; pharmacologic validation uses research-grade NF-κB/ROCK inhibitors rather than a clinical compound, and the paper was published 2026-03-02 so this is backlog ingestion rather than a fresh-this-week event.

**Classification:** hypothesis-generating
**Study design:** preclinical-animal (LEC-conditional KO mice + pharmacologic rescue)
**Publication event:** full-paper-published (J Clin Invest, 2026-03-02; article date 2026-01-13)
**Endpoint:** exploratory — valve morphology, FOXC2 expression, vessel patterning
**Comparator:** wild-type / Cre-only / LEC-Foxc2 KO littermates
**Result:** positive (converging genetic + pharmacologic evidence)
**Skepticism flags:** Stale relative to the 14-day digest window (is_new_this_week=false); animal-only evidence; no human validation.
