## Overview: Primary vs. Secondary Lymphedema

**Primary lymphedema** arises from genetic abnormalities in lymphatic development. It accounts for approximately 1% of adult lymphedema cases and is rare (~1/100,000 children). It can be congenital (present at birth), praecox (onset at puberty to age 35), or tarda (onset after 35).

**Secondary lymphedema** results from damage to a normally developed lymphatic system. It accounts for ~99% of adult cases. The dominant causes are:
- **Lymphatic filariasis** (globally): 70+ million people affected
- **Cancer surgery** (high-income countries): axillary node dissection for breast cancer (29–49% incidence after ALND), pelvic/inguinal dissections
- **Radiation therapy**
- **Obesity**
- **Trauma and infection**

Understanding genetic architecture is primarily relevant for primary lymphedema. However, genetic factors also modify susceptibility to secondary lymphedema — for example, polymorphisms near the *FLT4* gene have been associated with risk of BCRL after axillary surgery.

**Tissue-level pathophysiology may differ between primary and secondary disease (W24 2026 update):** A single-center case-control study (*Lymphatic Research and Biology*, 2026; 15 primary-lymphedema patients vs 8 controls, biopsies of affected and unaffected limbs) reported *reduced* cutaneous fibrosis in primary lymphedema — the **opposite** of the fibrosis-dominated picture seen in secondary/BCRL lymphedema — together with downregulation of *CLDN5* (claudin-5) and *VEGFD*, and decreased *TJP1* (ZO-1, a tight-junction protein) in affected but not unaffected limbs. The authors interpret this as an endothelial **barrier / permeability** defect rather than fibrotic lymphatic obstruction. *If replicated, this would mechanistically separate primary from secondary lymphedema and shift therapeutic thinking away from the anti-fibrotic strategies borrowed from BCRL.* For now it is a single small study (n=15) using molecular surrogates with no functional correlation — hypothesis-generating only.

## The VEGF-C / VEGFR-3 Axis: The Central Molecular Story

The most important pathway in lymphedema genetics and biology:

1. **VEGF-C** (Vascular Endothelial Growth Factor C) is produced as an inactive precursor protein. It requires processing by the metalloprotease **ADAMTS3** (facilitated by the extracellular matrix protein **CCBE1**) to become the active growth factor.
2. Active VEGF-C binds **VEGFR-3** (encoded by *FLT4*) on lymphatic endothelial cells (LECs).
3. VEGFR-3 homodimers activate ERK1/2 (MAPK) → LEC proliferation and survival
4. VEGFR-3/VEGFR-2 heterodimers activate PI3K → AKT → LEC migration and tube formation
5. Downstream transcription factors include **PROX1** (the "master gene" of LEC identity) and **FOXC2** (critical for lymphatic valve formation)

Mutations anywhere along this axis cause primary lymphedema:
- *FLT4* (VEGFR-3): Milroy disease
- *VEGFC*: rare cases of primary lymphedema
- *CCBE1* and *ADAMTS3*: Hennekam syndrome (block VEGF-C processing)
- *FOXC2*: Lymphedema-distichiasis syndrome (downstream of VEGFR-3)
- *PROX1*: rare primary lymphedema (novel mutations still being discovered as of 2025)

This axis is also the rationale for VEGF-C gene therapy (Lymfactin) as a potential treatment — delivering VEGF-C to stimulate new lymphatic growth.

## Key Genes and Syndromes

### FLT4 / VEGFR-3 — Milroy Disease

**Syndrome:** Milroy disease (hereditary lymphedema type I, OMIM 153100)

**Inheritance:** Autosomal dominant

**Mutations:** Inactivating missense mutations in the intracellular tyrosine kinase domain of VEGFR-3. These mutations produce a dominant-negative protein — the mutant receptor not only fails to signal but also impairs signaling by normal VEGFR-3 from the unaffected allele.

**Clinical features:** Bilateral lower limb edema present at birth or within the first two years of life. Males tend to have more severe involvement. Associated findings include genital edema, upslanting toenails, papillomatosis of the feet, and occasional hydrocele (in males).

**Genetic penetrance:** ~80% penetrance in most series — not all carriers develop clinically apparent lymphedema.

**Key fact:** VEGFR-3 pathway mutations collectively explain approximately 36% of familial lymphedema.

**W31 2026 addition — 2 more novel variants from prenatal cases:** A small case series (*Birth Defects Research*, 2023) reported two further novel FLT4 variants — p.(Ser1275Thr) and p.(Ser1275Arg) — identified prenatally in two fetuses with congenital lymphedema (hereditary lymphedema type 1), reviewed alongside prior prenatally-confirmed FLT4 cases in the literature. This is variant-catalog evidence only (n=2), with no functional characterization reported.

**Emerging mechanism — VEGFR-3 / HSPG2 (perlecan) interaction (W24 2026, preprint):** A bioRxiv preprint (2026) reports that the physical interaction between VEGFR-3 and the heparan-sulfate proteoglycan **HSPG2/perlecan** is required for normal lymphatic function in an animal model; disrupting it caused lymphatic reflux dysfunction and sepsis, nominating HSPG2 as a novel VEGFR-3 binding partner potentially relevant to Milroy biology. *This is an unreviewed preprint in an animal model — tracked for the VEGFR-3 connection, but no clinical inference is warranted.*

### FOXC2 — Lymphedema-Distichiasis Syndrome

**Syndrome:** Lymphedema-distichiasis (OMIM 153400)

**Inheritance:** Autosomal dominant

**Mutations:** Loss-of-function mutations (frameshift, nonsense, splice site) in FOXC2

**What FOXC2 does:** FOXC2 is a forkhead transcription factor essential for lymphatic valve morphogenesis and for maintaining LEC identity. Without functional FOXC2, lymphatic valves fail to form properly, causing retrograde lymph flow (backflow) and progressive edema.

**Clinical features:**
- **Distichiasis**: An extra row of eyelashes growing from the meibomian gland openings — the pathognomonic feature. It causes chronic eye irritation.
- **Bilateral lower limb lymphedema**: Usually onset at puberty, later than Milroy disease
- **Associated findings**: ptosis (droopy eyelid), congenital heart defects (in ~7%), varicose veins (in ~49%), ectopic cilia
- **Penetrance**: Near-complete for distichiasis (~95%); lower for lymphedema (~80%)

**2026 update — upstream regulation of FOXC2:** Yasuda et al. (*Journal of Clinical Investigation*, January 2026) identified a new extracellular regulatory layer above FOXC2. The bioactive lipid **lysophosphatidic acid (LPA)** — acting on its GPCR receptors **LPA4** and **LPA6** — drives FOXC2 expression in lymphatic endothelial cells via a **Gα12/Gα13 → Rho-kinase → NF-κB** signaling axis. LEC-specific deletion of *Lpa4* and *Lpa6* in mice phenocopies LEC-specific *Foxc2* deletion, with impaired lymphatic valve formation and maintenance. Pharmacologic inhibition of NF-κB or Rho-kinase impaired valve maintenance in the same model. Until this work, only oscillatory shear stress was known to induce FOXC2.

*What this means for patients:* The finding is mouse-only, mechanistic, and **does not change clinical care for lymphedema-distichiasis today**. It does, however, identify several druggable nodes (Rho-kinase, NF-κB, the LPA-receptor axis) that could in principle be tested for the ability to rescue valve function in hypomorphic FOXC2 settings. Lymphedema preclinical research has a poor translation track record — patients should expect this kind of finding to be a 5+ year hypothesis statement, not a near-term clinical lead. *Confidence: low (mouse-only; preclinical translation warning).*

### GJC2 (Connexin 47) — Lymphedema Praecox

**Syndrome:** Lymphedema praecox type II / Meige disease subtype (OMIM 613480)

**Inheritance:** Autosomal dominant

**Mutations:** Missense mutations in GJC2

**What GJC2 does:** GJC2 encodes connexin 47, a gap junction protein expressed in LECs. Gap junctions allow direct cytoplasmic communication between adjacent cells. In lymphatic vessels, GJC2-mediated gap junctions are required for coordinated lymphangion contractions and proper valve function.

**Clinical features:**
- Bilateral lower limb edema onset at puberty to age 35
- No systemic features (distinguishes from FLT4 and FOXC2 syndromes)
- More variable expressivity than Milroy disease

**2026 update:** Shi et al. (*Orphanet Journal of Rare Diseases*, January 2026) reported a novel GJC2 missense variant c.287G>T (p.Gly96Val) in the second transmembrane domain of connexin 47, co-segregating with autosomal-dominant generalized primary lymphedema across a four-generation Chinese pedigree (whole-exome sequencing + Sanger in 26 relatives). The same report identified a co-segregating OBSCN variant c.22393T>A, which the authors proposed as a possible second contributor. *Interpretation:* the GJC2 variant is the biologically plausible causal change. OBSCN (obscurin) is a very large sarcomeric gene where rare missense variants are common in the general population, and single-family co-segregation is not sufficient evidence to add it to diagnostic panels. OBSCN should not be counseled as a lymphedema gene until independent replication appears.

### SOX18 — Hypotrichosis-Lymphedema-Telangiectasia

**Syndrome:** Hypotrichosis-lymphedema-telangiectasia (OMIM 607063)

**Inheritance:** Autosomal dominant (loss-of-function) OR autosomal recessive (more severe)

**What SOX18 does:** SOX18 is a transcription factor that acts upstream of PROX1, initiating the process by which venous endothelial cells are specified into LECs during embryonic development. SOX18 activates PROX1 expression in a subset of cardinal vein cells, which then bud off to form the primary lymph sacs.

**Clinical features:** Lymphedema, hypotrichosis (sparse hair and eyebrows), telangiectasias (dilated capillaries visible on the skin)

**2026 update — bleeding diathesis / low von Willebrand factor:** A case report (*AJMG Part A*, 2026) describes a 10-year-old boy with HLTS due to a heterozygous *SOX18* nonsense variant (c.481C>T, p.Gln161Ter) who had recurrent mucosal bleeding, hemostatic difficulty after tooth extraction, reduced **von Willebrand factor (vWF)** and impaired platelet agglutination — but **no** *VWF* gene mutation. Because SOX18 regulates vWF expression, the low vWF appears secondary to SOX18 dysfunction; vWF replacement enabled safe dental procedures. *Practical counseling point (single-case evidence):* consider assessing vWF / coagulation before elective or dental procedures in SOX18-related HLTS.

### GATA2 — Emberger Syndrome

**Syndrome:** GATA2 deficiency / Emberger syndrome (OMIM 614038)

**Inheritance:** Autosomal dominant (de novo or familial)

**What GATA2 does:** GATA2 is a zinc finger transcription factor critical for hematopoiesis (blood cell formation) and lymphatic development. It acts upstream of PROX1 and FOXC2 in the lymphatic developmental program.

**Clinical features:** This is a complex multi-system syndrome:
- Lower limb lymphedema (often the presenting feature)
- **Myelodysplastic syndrome (MDS)** or acute myeloid leukemia (AML) — the most dangerous complication, with ~75% lifetime risk
- **Immunodeficiency**: susceptibility to unusual infections (HPV, mycobacteria, EBV)
- **Sensorineural hearing loss**
- **Warts** (from HPV)

**Critical implication:** Any patient with primary lower limb lymphedema *and* unexplained cytopenias, infections, or warts should be evaluated for GATA2 deficiency. This is a medically important diagnosis requiring oncological surveillance and consideration of bone marrow transplantation.

**2026 update:** Dong et al. (*Journal of Clinical Immunology*, March 2026) reported three sporadic cases (variants c.818dupG and c.1084C>T) showing the heterogeneous phenotype of GATA2 deficiency: case 1 with recurrent pulmonary infection plus cytopenias; cases 2 and 3 with congenital sensorineural deafness. Although a small case series adds no new mechanism, it reinforces a practical diagnostic point — **sensorineural hearing loss alongside primary lymphedema should prompt GATA2 testing**, even in the absence of overt hematologic disease at presentation.

**W24 2026 update:** A further case (*Dermatology Online Journal*, 2026) reinforces the same message from the opposite direction — a 31-year-old woman whose **childhood-onset progressive primary lymphedema**, treatment-resistant warts, and severe **monocytopenia** led to the GATA2 diagnosis. Primary lymphedema can be the sentinel cutaneous sign that unmasks GATA2 deficiency; the combination of lymphedema with cytopenia, recurrent warts, or atypical infection should trigger GATA2 testing because of the downstream MDS/AML risk.

### KIF11 — Microcephaly-Chorioretinopathy-Lymphedema-Intellectual Disability (MCLMR)

**Syndrome:** MCLMR syndrome (OMIM 152950)

**Inheritance:** Autosomal dominant

**Mutations:** Loss-of-function mutations in KIF11 (Eg5 kinesin, a motor protein required for mitotic spindle formation)

**Clinical features:** Microcephaly, chorioretinopathy, cataracts, intellectual disability, and lower limb lymphedema. The multisystem phenotype reflects KIF11's fundamental role in cell division.

### Noonan Syndrome and Other RASopathies — Lymphatic Involvement

**Syndrome:** Noonan syndrome (OMIM 163950 and related entries) and the broader RASopathy family (Costello, cardiofaciocutaneous, Noonan-with-multiple-lentigines / LEOPARD, etc.).

**Inheritance:** Mostly autosomal dominant, often de novo. Affected genes include *PTPN11*, *SOS1*, *RAF1*, *KRAS*, *RIT1*, *NRAS*, *BRAF*, *MAP2K1/2*, and others — all converging on the RAS/MAPK pathway.

**Lymphatic phenotype:** Variable. Some Noonan patients present with **persistent fetal hydrops**, **chylothorax** (often refractory), **pulmonary lymphangiectasia**, **intestinal lymphangiectasia**, **lower-limb lymphedema**, or **central conducting lymphatic anomalies**. Lymphedema may onset in infancy, childhood, or adolescence.

**2026 update — central lymphatic abnormalities are common, including in patients without overt lymphedema:** Sleboda et al. (*American Journal of Medical Genetics Part A*, 2026) reported a controlled cross-sectional study comparing 10 Noonan-syndrome individuals (4 with peripheral lymphedema, 6 without) to 10 age- and sex-matched cardiovascularly healthy controls, using non-contrast T2 MR-lymphangiography, near-infrared fluorescence imaging, and strain-gauge plethysmography. **Eighty-nine percent (8/9) of Noonan individuals showed thoracic-duct morphology variations** vs 20% (2/10) of controls (p<0.05). **Central lymphatic abnormalities were identified in 5/9 Noonan individuals — including 3 without overt clinical lymphatic disease.** Abnormal peripheral collective vessel conduction (retrograde or absent flow) was observed only in Noonan individuals with overt lymphedema.

*What this means for patients and clinicians:* Central lymphatic anomalies appear common in Noonan syndrome and may be subclinical. The finding is most relevant for Noonan patients with chylothorax, refractory pleural or pericardial effusions, or planned cardiac surgery — central lymphatic imaging can reveal anatomy that informs management. The data **do not** yet justify routine MR-lymphangiography screening of all Noonan patients without lymphatic symptoms. Clinical signs (e.g., recurrent effusions, hydrops, persistent edema, intestinal lymphangiectasia) should still drive imaging decisions. *Confidence: low_to_moderate (small but controlled cross-sectional study; surrogate endpoint; no genotype-stratified analysis).*

**Mechanistic context:** RASopathy mutations activate the same RAS/MAPK pathway implicated in somatic *KRAS*-driven complex lymphatic anomalies (see "Emerging Genes and Pathways" below). The Bowles 2026 KRAS-G12D mouse data — showing valve loss via MMP upregulation — provide a candidate explanation for why germline RASopathy mutations also disrupt lymphatic valve and central-conducting-system development. This connection is mechanistic and not yet clinically actionable.

### PIEZO1 — Generalized Lymphatic Dysplasia

**Syndrome:** Generalized lymphatic dysplasia type 3 / hereditary lymphedema type 3 (OMIM 616843)

**Inheritance:** Autosomal recessive (gain-of-function mutations)

**What PIEZO1 does:** PIEZO1 is a mechanosensitive ion channel that detects fluid shear stress on lymphatic endothelial cells. When activated, it drives lymphatic vessel growth and valve formation via an Ang2/TIE/PI3K/AKT/FOXO1 cascade. Loss-of-function mutations impair this mechanosensory response.

**Clinical features:** Generalized lymphedema affecting all four limbs, face, and genitalia. Non-immune fetal hydrops (fluid accumulation in the fetus) can be severe. This is a rare cause of primary lymphedema.

**Research importance:** PIEZO1 is one of the most actively studied targets in lymphedema drug development as of 2026 — PIEZO1 agonists (Yoda1) have shown dramatic benefit in mouse lymphedema models. See the Pharmacological Treatments document.

**Mechanistic map is broadening (W32 2026 update):** Two further preclinical papers extend where PIEZO1 sits in lymphatic developmental signaling, without adding clinical evidence. A *Circulation Research* (2024) study using lymphatic-endothelial-specific EPHB4 knockin/knockout mice, plus human dermal lymphatic endothelial cells in vitro, found that EPHB4 and its partner RASA1 act upstream of PIEZO1-driven Ras signaling specifically during lymphatic **valve** formation — connecting two genes independently linked to lymphatic malformation syndromes (EPHB4-related lymphatic anomaly; RASA1-related capillary malformation-AVM) into one valvulogenesis pathway. Separately, single-cell RNA-seq work (conference abstract) reports a Piezo2–Vegfr3–Prox1 axis regulating lymphatic vessel growth during adipose-tissue expansion in mice, tying PIEZO-family mechanosensing to obesity-associated lymphatic dysfunction. Both are mouse/in-vitro mechanistic studies — they widen the map of PIEZO1's role in lymphatic biology but do not change the confidence or clinical-translation status of PIEZO1 as a drug target (still preclinical; see Pharmacological Treatments).

### CCBE1 and ADAMTS3 — Hennekam Syndrome

**Syndromes:** Hennekam lymphangiectasia-lymphedema syndrome types 1 (CCBE1, OMIM 235510) and 3 (ADAMTS3, OMIM 618154)

**Inheritance:** Autosomal recessive

**What they do:** CCBE1 and ADAMTS3 work together to process pro-VEGF-C into active VEGF-C. Without this processing step, VEGFR-3 cannot be activated, abolishing lymphangiogenesis.

**Clinical features:** Intestinal lymphangiectasia (protein-losing enteropathy), lymphedema, intellectual disability, facial dysmorphism, hypertelorism (wide-set eyes). A severe, multisystem disorder.

---

### FAT4 — Hennekam Syndrome 2

**Syndrome:** Hennekam lymphangiectasia-lymphedema syndrome type 2 (OMIM 616006)

**Inheritance:** Autosomal recessive

**What FAT4 does:** FAT4 encodes a large atypical cadherin involved in planar cell polarity signaling and Hippo-pathway regulation. Its role in lymphatic development is less well characterized than that of CCBE1/ADAMTS3 in Hennekam 1/3.

**Clinical features:** Overlapping with Hennekam type 1 — lymphedema, intestinal lymphangiectasia, facial dysmorphism, developmental delay — with variable expressivity. FAT4-associated disease is less common than CCBE1/ADAMTS3-associated Hennekam in the published literature.

**2026 update:** Mascarenhas & Gupta (*American Journal of Medical Genetics Part A*, April 2026) reported a novel biallelic splicing variant c.12479+3A>G in FAT4 in a 15-month-old with peripheral lymphedema, dysmorphic facies, and camptodactyly, expanding the known FAT4 allelic spectrum. This is a single case report and should be interpreted as variant-curation evidence rather than a practice change. *Confidence: very low (n=1; no in vitro splicing assay reported).*

**Inheritance:** Autosomal recessive

**Clinical features:** Lymphedema combined with choanal atresia (blockage of nasal passages), facial dysmorphism

## Emerging Genes and Pathways

The genetic architecture of primary lymphedema is incompletely understood. Known mutations explain approximately **36% of familial primary lymphedema** and only **~8% of sporadic primary lymphedema**. The remaining cases are genetically unexplained, suggesting many causative genes remain undiscovered.

Ongoing whole-exome and whole-genome sequencing studies are identifying novel candidates including:
- Variants in *ANGPT2* and *TIE1* (Angiopoietin/TIE receptor pathway) in primary lymphedema patients
- *PROX1* novel mutations (a 2025 case report in *Frontiers in Genetics* identified a novel PROX1 variant in primary lymphedema)
- *RASA1* variants (associated with capillary malformation-AVM syndrome with lymphedema)
- *TSC1/TSC2* (tuberous sclerosis with lymphangioleiomyomatosis)

The VEGF-C/VEGFR-3 pathway, angiopoietin-TIE pathway, PI3K/AKT pathway, and PIEZO1 mechanosensory pathway are all implicated by both genetic and functional evidence.

**New 2026 mechanistic candidates (preclinical, not yet linked to human disease):**
- **ERG and FLI1** (ETS transcription factors): Yang et al. (*Journal of Clinical Investigation*, March 2026) demonstrated that combined loss of both Erg and Fli1 in adult mice causes fatal lymphatic failure — chylothorax, chylous ascites, impaired drainage, and disrupted lymphatic valve gene programs. Single loss of either alone is non-lethal. These factors regulate CCL21-mediated lymphatic-immune crosstalk and overlap in their target genes with the established FOXC2/PROX1 regulatory network. Neither ERG nor FLI1 is currently classified as a human primary lymphedema gene, but this mouse model data makes them plausible candidate genes for genetic screening in unsolved familial lymphedema cases. *Confidence: very low (mouse model only; no human variant data reported).*
- **LPA4 / LPA6 GPCR axis upstream of FOXC2** (Yasuda 2026, *JCI*): see the FOXC2 / lymphedema-distichiasis section above. Mouse data places LPA4/LPA6 G-protein-coupled receptors as required upstream regulators of FOXC2 expression and lymphatic valve maintenance. *Confidence: very low (mouse model only).*
- **VEGFR3-HSPG2 (perlecan) basement-membrane axis** (preprint, ss_3c57b92fa67d, *bioRxiv* 2026): a mouse mechanistic model proposing that disruption of the VEGFR3-perlecan interaction impairs lymphatic reflux prevention and increases sepsis susceptibility. Relevant to VEGFR3 / Milroy biology because it adds a matrix-anchoring layer to receptor function. *Confidence: very low (preprint; abstract-only methods; awaiting peer review).*
- **KRAS-G12D → matrix metalloproteinase upregulation → lymphatic valve loss** (Bowles 2026, *Arteriosclerosis, Thrombosis, and Vascular Biology*): somatic activating *KRAS* mutations are recognised drivers of complex lymphatic anomalies (CLA), chylothorax, and chylous ascites. Using Flt4CreERT2;Kras-LSL-G12D;Prox1GFP mice and KRAS-G12D-expressing human dermal LECs, the authors showed that lymphatic-restricted KRAS-G12D significantly attenuates valve development across multiple anatomic sites and that KRAS-G12D LECs upregulate matrix metalloproteinases (MMPs). Pharmacologic MMP inhibition rescued valve formation in 3D culture, nominating MMPs as a downstream druggable axis in KRAS-mutant lymphatic disease. *What this means for patients:* Mechanistic only. No MMP inhibitor is currently in human lymphatic-disease trials, and broad MMP inhibition has historically been toxic in oncology. The current pharmacologic options for KRAS-mutant lymphatic anomalies remain RAS/MEK-pathway inhibitors (trametinib for selected RAS-mutant CLA) and supportive sclerotherapy/surgical care. *Confidence: very low (mouse model + 3D cell culture only).*

### RAS-MAPK Pathway in Non-Syndromic Primary Lymphedema — W22 2025 Addition

**Background:** The RAS/MAPK signaling pathway is well established in *syndromic* primary lymphedema. Germline mutations in *PTPN11*, *SOS1*, *RAF1*, *MAP2K1*, *KRAS*, and *LZTR1* cause Noonan syndrome and related RASopathies, some of which include lymphatic manifestations (see Noonan syndrome section above). Somatic RAS/MAPK mutations also drive complex lymphatic anomalies. However, prior to 2025, no study had reported RAS/MAPK pathway variants in clinically non-syndromic primary lymphedema patients.

**New 2025 data:** Belanova et al. (*Lymphology*, August 2025; PMID 40803040) retrospectively analyzed the same Italian cohort of 408 primary lymphedema patients and identified **five genetic variants** in **LZTR1**, **MAP2K1**, and **RAF1** — four of which had not been previously reported. In silico molecular modeling supported potential pathogenicity of a RAF1 variant. Crucially, none of these patients carried a clinical Noonan syndrome diagnosis. A PubMed search returns zero prior publications linking LZTR1 to lymphedema in any context — this constitutes a first report.

*What this suggests:* RAS/MAPK pathway activation below the threshold required for full Noonan syndrome phenotype may nonetheless be sufficient to disrupt lymphatic development, producing isolated primary lymphedema. If replicated, this would blur the diagnostic boundary between syndromic and non-syndromic primary lymphedema, with implications for genetic panel design: patients with panel-negative non-syndromic primary lymphedema and RAS/MAPK variants of uncertain significance may have supporting (though weak) literature after 2025.

*What limits confidence:* Retrospective design; pathogenicity is computational only — no functional validation (cell culture, animal model) has been reported for any of the five variants; single Italian cohort with potential founder effects; very low allele count (5 variants across 408 patients, ~1.2% of the cohort); no clinical comparison between RAS/MAPK-positive and RAS/MAPK-negative primary lymphedema patients. Independent replication in a second cohort is required before these genes are considered candidate diagnostic panel additions.

**Watchlist:** Independent replication in a non-Italian cohort. Functional validation of any of the five variants in cell or animal models. *Confidence: low (hypothesis-generating; single retrospective cohort; computational pathogenicity only).*

### HGF-MET Pathway in Primary Lymphedema — W22 2025 Update

**Background:** The hepatocyte growth factor (HGF) receptor MET has been a candidate lymphedema gene since Finegold et al. (*New England Journal of Medicine*, 2008) identified HGF-MET signaling defects in primary lymphedema. Kajiya et al. (2005) established that HGF-MET regulates lymphangiogenesis in vitro. However, clinical genetic evidence for this pathway has been limited to small early series.

**New 2025 data:** Kozáčiková et al. (*Lymphology*, April 2025; PMID 40245437) conducted a dedicated genetic analysis of the HGF-MET pathway in the same Italian cohort of 408 primary lymphedema patients, identifying **8 variants** (7 previously unreported) in *HGF*, *MET*, and *CBL* (the latter encoding an E3 ubiquitin ligase that regulates MET degradation). This is the largest systematic evaluation of the HGF-MET pathway in primary lymphedema to date.

*Italian cohort context:* This paper is part of a multi-year systematic gene-discovery program by the same research group. Publications from this cohort include the 2018 baseline NGS panel (n=116), 2020 TIE1 and ARAP3 candidate papers, 2020 PROX1 variant paper, 2025 HGF-MET paper, and 2025 RAS-MAPK paper. The 408-patient cohort represents one of the largest systematically genotyped primary lymphedema populations in the published literature, but all papers come from the same group — independent replication from geographically distinct cohorts has not been published.

*What limits confidence:* Retrospective design; functional validation of variants absent — all pathogenicity inferences are computational; Italian cohort may have population-specific founder effects; the HGF-MET pathway is not currently included in standard clinical gene panels. *Confidence: low (extends prior art; computational only; single cohort).*

**Clinical relevance:** HGF-MET variants are not currently actionable for therapeutic decision-making. HGF-MET is a druggable target (MET inhibitors exist in oncology: cabozantinib, crizotinib), but no lymphedema-specific HGF-MET therapeutic studies exist. The pathway's relevance for primary lymphedema remains hypothesis-generating.

### Common-variant architecture: 2026 Million Veteran Program GWAS

Until 2026, the lymphedema field has relied almost entirely on Mendelian gene discovery (single-gene, single-family-driven). The first dedicated **multi-population genome-wide association study** of lymphedema and chronic venous insufficiency (CVI) was published by **Peloso et al. (*Circulation: Genomic and Precision Medicine*, April 2026)** using the Million Veteran Program. Cancer cases were excluded, increasing the relative weight of primary, idiopathic, and post-surgical-non-cancer lymphedema in the case definition (lymphedema-only n=2,452; lymphedema+CVI n=3,283; CVI-only n=34,664; controls n=367,684). Findings:

- **11 genome-wide significant variants** in the multi-population analysis: 8 CVI-only, 1 lymphedema-only, 2 lymphedema+CVI, plus 2 population-specific.
- **Three CVI-only loci replicated in the UK Biobank**: near *CASZ1*, *SLC12A2*, and *NDP*.
- **Polygenic risk scores** derived in MVP were associated with CVI in UKB; no equivalent independent replication of the lymphedema-only PRS is reported.
- **Phenome-wide pleiotropy** with cardiometabolic traits at CVI-associated loci.

*Interpretation for the primary-lymphedema field:* This is the first attempt to map a common-variant landscape adjacent to (and potentially overlapping with) the established Mendelian gene set (FLT4, FOXC2, GJC2, PIEZO1, etc.). The lymphedema-only locus and the two lymphedema+CVI loci warrant fine-mapping to determine which genes they tag and whether they connect to known lymphangiogenic pathways. *Limitations:* EHR phecode ascertainment is heterogeneous, the lymphedema-only PRS did not replicate in UKB, and primary lymphedema is undoubtedly a small minority of cases. **Polygenic risk scoring is not yet recommended for primary-lymphedema risk prediction or genetic counseling.** *Confidence: low_to_moderate.*

## Genetic Testing in Practice

### Who Should Consider Genetic Testing

- Patients with **primary lymphedema** (onset at birth or in childhood/adolescence without an obvious cause)
- Patients with associated features suggesting a syndrome (distichiasis, unusual infections, hearing loss, developmental delay, warts)
- Family members of patients with known genetic mutations
- Patients with **suspicion of GATA2 deficiency** (lymphedema + cytopenias + unusual infections) — this is medically urgent

### What Testing Is Available

The **University of Pennsylvania Lymphedema Gene Panel** and similar panels at other academic centers include 20+ genes: FLT4, FOXC2, GJC2, SOX18, PROX1, GATA2, KIF11, PIEZO1, CCBE1, ADAMTS3, PTPN14, VEGFC, ANGPT2, TIE1, and others. Whole-exome sequencing is increasingly accessible and cost-effective.

### Important Caveat

A negative genetic panel does not exclude primary lymphedema — many cases remain genetically unexplained. Conversely, finding a variant requires careful interpretation by a clinical geneticist, as many variants of uncertain significance (VUS) exist in these genes.

### Real-World Access and Yield (2026 cross-center analysis)

Genetic testing for vascular and lymphatic anomalies often requires somatic (lesion-tissue) sampling rather than germline blood/saliva, particularly for malformations driven by post-zygotic mutations (PIK3CA, KRAS, NRAS, BRAF, MAP2K1, GNAQ). A 2026 two-center retrospective analysis (n=421; *Blood Vessels, Thrombosis & Hemostasis*) provides the first systematic look at how this testing actually flows through prior authorization (PA):

- **PA was attempted in 61.7% of vascular-anomaly patients.** 84% approved, 93% completed, **70% positive (P/LP variants).**
- **Sample type and freshness did not affect yield** — frozen, formalin-fixed, and fresh samples performed comparably when the lab was experienced with the substrate.
- **Skin findings and male sex predicted PA initiation;** higher Area Deprivation Index (a measure of neighborhood-level socioeconomic disadvantage) reduced PA odds at one center.

*What this means for patients:* If a clinician suspects a somatic genetic cause for a vascular or lymphatic malformation and orders testing, the test is likely to yield an actionable result (~70%). Access barriers — including socioeconomic — are real. Patients should ask whether somatic (lesion-tissue) testing has been considered when germline panels are non-diagnostic, and whether a referral to a vascular-anomaly center is appropriate. Centers that perform somatic testing routinely have higher diagnostic yields than centers that do not.

### Variant of Uncertain Significance (VUS) Resolution: An Emerging Clinical Priority

The majority of FLT4 variants identified in suspected Milroy disease patients are classified as VUS and cannot be used for clinical decision-making in their current form. Functional characterization studies are beginning to address this:

- **Filina et al. 2026** (*Current Issues in Molecular Biology*, January 2026) used in vitro kinase activity assays to show that the FLT4 c.3175G>C (p.A1059P) variant has measurably decreased VEGFR3 kinase activity, supporting reclassification to likely pathogenic. A companion study from the same group identified a novel FLT4 c.3028A>C VUS as likely pathogenic in a cohort of 24 primary lymphedema patients, and provided supporting functional evidence for c.3175G>C and c.3298T>C.
- **Monaghan et al. 2024** (*Cardiovascular Research*) provided important context: FLT4 variants associated with Tetralogy of Fallot (TOF, a congenital heart defect) operate through a distinct molecular mechanism from FLT4 variants causing Milroy disease. TOF-associated variants cause protein aggregation in the endoplasmic reticulum; lymphedema-associated variants do not. This mechanistic separation explains why the clinical phenotypes are non-overlapping despite sharing the same gene — and has implications for genetic counseling: variant interpretation should consider which mechanism (ER aggregation vs. kinase impairment) the specific variant exhibits.

*What this means for patients:* If you have received a "VUS" result on FLT4/VEGFR3, this does not mean the variant is harmless — it means the evidence is currently insufficient to classify it definitively. Functional studies like the ones above are gradually resolving this uncertainty for specific variants. Asking your genetic counselor about functional assay availability for your specific variant is reasonable as of 2026.

*Current limitation:* Most FLT4 VUS remain uncharacterized. This work is incremental — a few variants per year are being resolved, but the backlog is large.

## Biomarkers: Measuring and Monitoring Lymphedema

Unlike diseases with single serum biomarkers (creatinine for kidney function, HbA1c for diabetes), lymphedema biomarker assessment is multi-modal. No single test captures the full picture.

### Limb Volume Measurement — The Fundamental Measure

**Water displacement volumetry**: Submerging the limb in a water-filled container and measuring displaced volume. The gold standard for absolute accuracy but inconvenient for routine use.

**Perometry**: Infrared optoelectronic scanning using a ring of infrared sensors to calculate limb circumference at multiple levels, then compute volume. Non-contact, reproducible, and suitable for clinical follow-up. Differences >10% between affected and unaffected limb, or >200 mL absolute difference, are considered clinically significant in most studies.

**Tape measure circumferences**: Low-tech but requires careful technique and standardized measurement points. Used in lower-resource settings and for simple monitoring.

### Bioimpedance Spectroscopy (BIS) — Early Detection

**What it measures**: BIS passes a small electrical current through the limb and measures impedance (resistance to current flow). Extracellular fluid (the edema fluid in lymphedema) has different electrical properties than intracellular fluid, allowing quantification of fluid accumulation. The L-Dex score compares the affected to unaffected limb.

**Why it matters**: BIS can detect subclinical lymphedema (ISL Stage 0 — elevated fluid before visible swelling) months before it becomes clinically apparent. Multiple prospective trials in post-mastectomy patients are using BIS for early detection, allowing intervention before overt edema develops. A BIS L-Dex score >7.1 is the most commonly used threshold for detecting subclinical BCRL.

### ICG Near-Infrared Lymphography — Functional Imaging and Staging

**What it does**: Indocyanine green (ICG) is a fluorescent dye injected intradermally in the web spaces of the fingers or toes. Near-infrared light illuminates the ICG as it travels through lymphatic vessels, providing real-time visualization of lymph flow.

**Why it matters**:
1. **Superior sensitivity**: ICG detects lymphatic dysfunction in 100% of symptomatic limbs vs. ~70% with conventional lymphoscintigraphy
2. **Surgical planning**: The ICG lymphography pattern directly guides surgical decision-making:
   - **Linear pattern** (Stage I–II): Normal-appearing lymphatic collectors with defined flow → excellent candidate for LVA surgery (61% average volume reduction in Stage I–II)
   - **Splash/stardust pattern** (Stage III–IV): Dermal backflow, absent collectors → less suitable for LVA; may prefer VLNT
   - **Diffuse pattern** (Stage V): Extensive dermal backflow → advanced disease; limited surgical options

**Limitations**: ICG lymphography is a near-surface technique; it does not visualize deep lymphatic trunks well. Requires specialized equipment (near-infrared cameras).

### Conventional Lymphoscintigraphy

**What it does**: Radioactive technetium-labeled albumin or colloid is injected into the limb; gamma camera imaging tracks the tracer through the lymphatic system over hours.

**Role**: Gold standard **functional** lymphatic imaging, providing information about lymphatic transport speed, nodal uptake, and overall lymphatic drainage capacity. Detected lymphatic dysfunction in ~70% of symptomatic limbs in one comparison (vs. 100% for ICG).

**Limitations**: Radiation exposure, requires nuclear medicine department, poorer spatial resolution than ICG, takes several hours.

### MRI

**What it does**: High-resolution MRI characterizes the tissue composition of the lymphedematous limb — quantifying edema fluid, adipose tissue, fibrosis, and skin thickness.

**Role**: Particularly useful for: (a) distinguishing edema from fibroadipose deposition (important for surgical planning — if mostly fatty tissue, liposuction may be appropriate); (b) detecting early fibrosis at high-frequency settings; (c) measuring skin thickness as a fibrosis biomarker.

**MR thoracic ductography (MRTD) and central lymphatic imaging:** Liu et al. (*Vascular Medicine*, February 2026) reported MRTD findings in 279 primary lower-extremity lymphedema patients stratified by ISL stage (I/II/III) — one of the largest primary-lymphedema-only imaging cohorts published. The study proposes imaging correlates of clinical stage for the thoracic duct, cisterna chyli, and inguinal drainage. *Limits:* cross-sectional; surrogate endpoint; no linkage to treatment-outcome data yet. MRTD is not a standard diagnostic step in most centers but is gaining traction for pre-operative characterization of central lymphatic anatomy, particularly in generalized lymphatic dysplasia and suspected central conducting lymphatic anomalies.

**Extension to syndromic primary lymphatic disease (Noonan, 2026):** Sleboda et al. (*American Journal of Medical Genetics Part A*, 2026) used non-contrast T2 MR-lymphangiography, NIRF imaging, and strain-gauge plethysmography in 10 Noonan-syndrome individuals vs 10 age/sex-matched controls. 89% of Noonan individuals showed thoracic-duct morphology variations vs 20% of controls (p<0.05); central lymphatic abnormalities were detected in 5/9 Noonan individuals **including 3 without overt clinical lymphatic disease**. The Liu (non-syndromic, n=279) and Sleboda (Noonan, n=10) cohorts are very different in size and design, but together they support the broader claim that **central lymphatic morphology is a sensitive imaging readout in primary lymphatic biology**, not just in non-syndromic primary lymphedema. Outcome-linked validation and inter-rater reproducibility remain unaddressed.

**Nonenhanced MRI grading — 2025 data:** Guo et al. (*Journal of Vascular Surgery: Venous and Lymphatic Disorders*, 2025) demonstrated in a retrospective single-center series of **124 patients** that nonenhanced MRI can effectively grade primary lower extremity lymphedema using specific imaging signs (subcutaneous fat diameter measurements and characteristic tissue signal patterns). This adds to the growing evidence that contrast-free MRI can serve as a staging and serial-monitoring tool in primary lymphedema without radiation or invasive lymphoscintigraphy. *Limits: retrospective single-center; external validation absent; no linkage to functional outcomes. Confidence: low_to_moderate.*

### High-Frequency Ultrasound

Measures skin thickness and echogenicity (tissue texture). Increased skin thickness and reduced echogenicity correlate with fibrosis and disease severity. Used in clinical research and some specialized centers.

## Predictive Biomarkers: Who Will Develop Lymphedema?

Identifying which cancer patients will develop lymphedema before it happens — enabling preventive intervention — is a major research priority.

### Pre-Surgical Plasma Cytokine Panel

A 2023 *Cancers* (MDPI) study identified a panel of plasma cytokines measured **before** axillary lymph node dissection (ALND) that predicted subsequent BCRL development. Key cytokines significantly elevated in patients who went on to develop BCRL included: **G-CSF, GM-CSF, IFN-2α, IL-10, IL-12p40, IL-15, IL-17A, IL-1β, IL-2, IL-3, IL-6, and MIP-1β**.

The implication: some women have a pre-existing systemic inflammatory state that makes them more susceptible to developing lymphedema after surgery. This could eventually allow stratification of patients into higher- and lower-risk groups before surgery, enabling tailored preventive approaches.

### Machine Learning Risk Models

A 2025 *Frontiers in Oncology* paper reported a machine learning model integrating preoperative clinical variables (BMI, age, extent of planned surgery, prior radiation history, comorbidities) to predict BCRL with superior accuracy compared to single-variable models. These models could inform decisions about ILR/LYMPHA surgery (see Disease Management).

### Bioimpedance Surveillance as Stage 0 Detection

Multiple prospective trials are evaluating BIS-based surveillance protocols: measuring L-Dex scores at baseline (before breast cancer surgery) and at regular intervals afterward. Early intervention triggered by subclinical BIS elevation — before overt edema — appears to prevent progression to Stage I in some studies.

## Summary

**For primary lymphedema patients:**
- Genetic testing is appropriate and should include a comprehensive gene panel (20+ genes)
- The most clinically important specific diagnosis to not miss is **GATA2 deficiency** (Emberger syndrome) — it requires oncological surveillance
- A negative panel does not exclude primary lymphedema; ~60–92% of cases remain genetically unexplained
- Secondary pathways (RAS-MAPK, HGF-MET, Angiopoietin-TIE) are present at low prevalence (~1–3% each) in the best-characterized cohort (Italian, n=408); whole-exome sequencing should be considered in panel-negative cases

**The central molecular pathway:**
- VEGF-C → ADAMTS3/CCBE1 processing → VEGFR-3 activation → PROX1 + FOXC2 → lymphatic vessel and valve formation
- Mutations anywhere along this axis cause primary lymphedema
- PIEZO1 mechanosensory pathway is an additional critical regulator and drug target

**For monitoring lymphedema:**
- Volume measurement (perometry or tape measure): essential for tracking progression and treatment response
- Bioimpedance (L-Dex): best for subclinical detection in post-surgical patients
- ICG lymphography: best for surgical planning and early-stage functional assessment
- Lymphoscintigraphy: standard functional imaging where ICG unavailable
- No single serum biomarker currently equivalent to creatinine for kidney disease
