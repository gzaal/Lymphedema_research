## Trial Landscape Overview

Lymphedema has historically been underserved by clinical trial activity — a reflection of its historical classification as a manageable complication rather than a disease requiring systematic study. That is changing rapidly:

- Multiple Phase II and Phase III trials are now recruiting
- Major interest areas: (1) surgical prevention (ILR/LYMPHA), (2) gene therapy (VEGF-C), (3) device-based early detection, (4) pharmacological approaches (GLP-1 agonists, anti-inflammatory agents)
- Breast cancer-related lymphedema (BCRL) dominates the trial landscape due to its high incidence and the well-defined post-surgical window for intervention
- Most current trials are in Phase I or II; no Phase III pharmacological trial equivalent to TEMPO 3:4 (for ADPKD) or similar landmark trials exists yet in lymphedema

**Key registries to search:** ClinicalTrials.gov (search "lymphedema"), ISRCTN, EudraCT (EU Clinical Trials Register)

## Gene Therapy Trials

### Lymfactin (Adenoviral VEGF-C) — Herantis Pharma

**Background:** Lymfactin delivers the VEGF-C gene via an adenoviral vector to stimulate lymphangiogenesis — growth of new lymphatic vessels.

**Phase I (COMPLETED, 2025):**
- Established safety and tolerability in BCRL patients
- No serious adverse events attributable to gene therapy
- Results published in ScienceDirect, 2025

**Phase II randomized trial (PUBLISHED, Plastic and Reconstructive Surgery, 2024):**
- Design: 39 BCRL patients randomized to Lymfactin + VLNT surgery vs. placebo + VLNT
- Primary endpoint (main severity measure): NOT MET at 12 months
- Secondary endpoint (tissue dielectric constant ratio): **Significant improvement** in Lymfactin group (P = 0.020)
- Safety: No difference between groups
- **Interpretation**: Biological proof-of-concept; underpowered; 12-month follow-up may be insufficient for lymphangiogenesis to mature

**Status as of April 2026:** Planning for next steps (Phase III or next-generation delivery) underway

## Surgical Intervention Trials

### Immediate Lymphatic Reconstruction (ILR) / LYMPHA

**Background:** ILR, also called LYMPHA (Lymphatic Microsurgical Preventing Healing Approach), involves performing miniature anastomoses (connections) between the divided lymphatic vessels and nearby small veins **at the time of axillary node dissection** — before lymphedema has a chance to develop. This prevents the lymphatic pressure buildup that leads to lymphedema.

**13-Year Meta-Analysis (PMC12076565, 2025):**
- Pooled data from multiple centers
- **ILR/LYMPHA reduced BCRL risk by ~64–75%** compared to controls (ALND without ILR)
- In one large series: 14.6% lymphedema rate with LYMPHA vs. 40% controls over 13 years
- Growing consensus that this should be offered to all patients undergoing level III ALND

**Current active trials:**
- Multiple prospective trials at major oncology centers evaluating:
  - Optimal anastomosis technique (LVA vs. LVV at time of ALND)
  - ILR at sentinel lymph node biopsy level (lower-risk procedure — can even this level benefit from prophylactic LVA?)
  - Axillary Reverse Mapping (ARM) combined with ILR
  - Long-term durability beyond 5 years

**Key questions:**
1. Which patients benefit most from ILR? Can pre-surgical risk stratification (cytokine panels, ML models) identify the highest-risk patients?
2. Does ILR at sentinel node biopsy level have sufficient benefit to justify the added operative time?
3. What are the minimal technical requirements for LYMPHA to be effective?

### Lymphaticovenular Anastomosis (LVA) for Established Lymphedema

**Background:** LVA is a supermicrosurgical procedure creating connections between small lymphatic collectors and adjacent venules, bypassing the obstructed lymphatic segments. Best results in early lymphedema (ISL Stage I–IIa, ICG Stage I–II linear pattern).

**Active trials:**
- Multiple prospective cohort and RCT designs at specialized lymphedema surgery centers
- Key outcomes: limb volume reduction at 12–24 months, compression garment discontinuation rates, quality of life
- Comparison trials: LVA vs. VLNT vs. combined approaches
- Technical development: supermicrosurgery techniques, imaging-guided anastomosis selection

**Best outcomes data (2024–2025):**
- Early disease (ICG Stage I–II): ~61% mean volume reduction at 2 years
- Late disease (ICG Stage III–IV): ~17% mean volume reduction (limited benefit)
- Garment-free rates: 25–40% at 2 years in early-stage disease with experienced surgeons

### Vascularized Lymph Node Transfer (VLNT)

**Background:** VLNT transfers healthy lymph nodes (with their arterial and venous blood supply) to the affected limb, creating a new drainage hub and potentially stimulating neo-lymphangiogenesis.

**Active trials:**
- Comparison of donor sites: inguinal vs. lateral thoracic vs. submental vs. gastroepiploic — which causes least donor-site lymphedema risk?
- Combination LVA + VLNT: evidence of synergy from case series; Phase II trials ongoing
- Vascularized lymph vessel transfer (VLVT) as alternative with lower donor-site risk

### Vascularized Lymph Vessel Transfer (VLVT)

An emerging alternative to VLNT that transfers the lymphatic vessels themselves (rather than nodes), potentially with lower donor-site complication risk. Currently in early clinical evaluation.

## Pharmacological Trials

### GLP-1 Receptor Agonists (Semaglutide / Tirzepatide)

**Design:** Observational and RCT designs exploring whether GLP-1 agonist-induced weight loss translates to meaningful lymphedema improvement, and whether there is a direct lymphatic biological effect beyond weight loss.

**Lead sites:** UCSD and collaborating centers
**Status:** Recruiting as of early 2026
**Expected initial results:** 2026–2027

### Ubenimex (LTB4 Inhibitor) — ULTRA Trial

**NCT02700529** — Phase 2 trial of ubenimex (LTB4 receptor antagonist) for lower limb lymphedema
**Status:** Completed; results **inconclusive**
**Interpretation:** Either LTB4 inhibition at this dose/duration is insufficient, or the trial was underpowered. No Phase III announced as of April 2026.

### Sirolimus for Lymphatic Anomalies

**NCT05835999 and related:** Multicenter prospective trials of sirolimus for complex cystic lymphatic anomalies (distinct from standard lymphedema)
**Status:** Ongoing; 2024 *Frontiers in Medicine* paper showed 50–80% symptom response rate
**Note:** Sirolimus is **not** being trialed for standard secondary lymphedema — its role is specifically for lymphatic malformations and PIK3CA-related overgrowth syndromes.

### RLY-2608 for PIK3CA-Related Overgrowth Spectrum — "ReInspire" (added W33 2026)

**NCT06789913.** Phase 2, **recruiting**. A mutant-selective PI3Kα inhibitor in adults and children with PIK3CA-related overgrowth spectrum (PROS) and malformations driven by PIK3CA mutation. Registered conditions explicitly include **lymphatic malformations**, vascular malformations, CLOVES syndrome, Klippel-Trenaunay syndrome and MCAP. Enrollment target 277; started June 2025; **primary completion July 2031**.

**Sponsor:** Relay Therapeutics, Inc. — *this is the company that makes the compound. Treat any efficacy messaging from the sponsor accordingly.*

**What to expect, realistically:** the **primary endpoint is dose-finding** (determining a recommended Phase 2 dose), not efficacy. Combined with a 2031 primary completion date, this means **no meaningful efficacy readout should be anticipated in the near term**. It is included here as a monitoring entry because it represents the mutant-selective generation of PI3Kα inhibitors entering a lymphatic-anomaly population — a genuine pipeline development relative to alpelisib — not because results are near.

### GATA2 Deficiency Natural History Study (added W33 2026)

**NCT01905826.** Observational natural history study, **recruiting**, sponsored by the US National Institute of Allergy and Infectious Diseases. Enrollment target 600; running since 2013, completion March 2028. Relevant to primary lymphedema through **Emberger syndrome**, in which childhood-onset primary lymphedema can be the presenting sign of GATA2 deficiency with its downstream risk of myelodysplastic syndrome and leukemia.

**Important expectation-setting:** this is an observational cohort, not a treatment trial. It will never produce an efficacy result. Its value is in characterising the full phenotype spectrum and the reasons for the striking variability between affected individuals — useful for counselling, not for therapy.

## Prevention Trials

### Bioimpedance Surveillance Protocols

Multiple prospective trials are testing whether measuring L-Dex scores (bioimpedance) at baseline and regular intervals after breast cancer surgery, with early CDT intervention triggered by subclinical elevation, prevents progression to clinical Stage I lymphedema.

**Design:** Pre-surgical baseline → surveillance every 3–6 months post-surgery → intervention if L-Dex > threshold before clinically apparent edema

**Evidence status:** Several RCTs and prospective cohorts published; overall evidence suggests subclinical intervention can prevent 30–50% of cases that would otherwise progress to Stage I

### Axillary Reverse Mapping (ARM)

ARM identifies the lymphatic drainage from the arm during breast cancer axillary surgery, allowing these vessels to be preserved or reconstructed while still removing cancer-bearing nodes. Ongoing technical refinement and prospective trials.

### Compression Garment Prescribing Post-Axillary Surgery

Trials evaluating whether immediate post-surgical compression (before any lymphedema develops) reduces BCRL incidence. Evidence emerging; not yet standard of care in all centers.

## Diagnostic and Biomarker Trials

### ICG Lymphography + AI Classification

**Deep learning models for ICG pattern classification:** Several groups developing automated algorithms to classify ICG lymphography patterns (linear/splash/stardust/diffuse) for surgical planning. A 2025 *Scientific Reports* paper reported high-accuracy deep learning classification of lower limb edema types.

**Why it matters:** ICG interpretation currently requires expert surgeons; automation could democratize access to optimal surgical planning.

### ML-Based BCRL Risk Prediction

**2025 Frontiers in Oncology paper:** Machine learning model integrating pre-operative variables (BMI, age, planned surgery extent, radiation history) for BCRL prediction, outperforming single-variable models. Could enable pre-surgical risk stratification to guide ILR/LYMPHA decisions.

### LIMPRINT II

The **LIMPRINT** (Lymphedema IMpact and PRevalence INTernational) study group is continuing its international epidemiological work, with national LIMPRINT databases active in Japan, Canada, and other countries. These provide real-world prevalence, severity, and treatment data.

## Completed Landmark Trials

### ILR/LYMPHA Meta-Analysis (2025)

Published in PMC12076565. 13-year data from multiple centers. Key finding: ILR at time of ALND reduces BCRL rate from ~40% to ~14.6% over 13 years. Represents the strongest long-term prevention data in the field.

### Lymfactin Phase II (2024–2025)

As described above. Mixed results but first randomized evidence that a drug affects a tissue-level biomarker (dielectric constant ratio) of lymphedema.

### ULTRA Trial (Ubenimex) — Completed, Inconclusive

Phase 2 RCT of LTB4 receptor antagonism; no clear signal of efficacy.

### Various CDT Optimization Trials

Multiple RCTs comparing CDT protocols, compression garment classes, number of MLD sessions per week, etc. The core finding: intensive CDT followed by maintenance compression remains the most effective non-surgical treatment.

## What to Watch

**12–24 months (through mid-2027):**

1. **Lymfactin next steps**: Will Herantis Pharma or a partner announce a Phase III trial, or pivot to next-generation VEGF-C mRNA delivery? Watch for press releases and publication updates.

2. **GLP-1 agonist trial results**: First data from semaglutide/tirzepatide trials expected 2026–2027. If significant, this would immediately change clinical practice given these drugs are already widely prescribed.

3. **PIEZO1 agonist IND filing**: If any pharmaceutical company or academic group announces an IND application for a PIEZO1 agonist for lymphedema, it would mark a new era.

4. **ILR/LYMPHA standardization**: Growing evidence will likely lead to formal incorporation of ILR into surgical oncology guidelines for ALND, representing a major prevention opportunity.

5. **Bioimpedance surveillance adoption**: Evidence accumulation may reach the threshold for universal post-ALND bioimpedance surveillance recommendations.

6. **Novel gene identification**: Whole-genome sequencing studies in primary lymphedema families are actively running; discovery of new causative genes could open new therapeutic directions.
